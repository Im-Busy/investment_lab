Page 1: 1. Introduction
Page 1: With the development of the behavioral finance, many scholars seek to explain the causes of stock market crashes using behavioral
Page 1: theories. For instance, Avery and Zemsky (1998) point out that herding can lead to mispricing of assets and price bubbles. Kim et al.
Page 1: (2016) suggest that firms with overconfident CEOs have higher stock price crash risk than other firms. Jang and Kang (2019) find that
Page 1: price crashes could be caused by the overpricing driven by institutional investors. While existing studies focus on exploring the
Page 1: influential factors that may lead to stock market crashes or predicting crash events in stock markets, we investigate how investors can
Page 1: profit from those events.
Page 1: Based on the aforementioned studies, a price crash can be caused by investorsΓÇÖ cognitive biases, such as herding bias and over┬¡
Page 1: confidence bias. Even though such cognitive biases can result in mispricing of securities, investors may theoretically expect excess
Page 1: returns generated from a stock market crash as long as they rationally use certain behavioral deviations (Conrad et al., 2014; Jang and
Page 1: Kang, 2019; Pelster, 2020). Therefore, it is highly possible to realize excess returns from a stock market crash.
Page 1: During a stock market crash, stock prices typically move in a similar pattern. First, stock prices continue to rise for an extended
Page 1: period until the bubble reaches its peak. Then, the bubble bursts and the market collapses with a sudden slump. If one wants to profit
Page 1: from a stock market crash, the ability to select stocks and set the timing is critical. Accordingly, this paper adopts the behavioral
Page 1: finance approach to propose two quantitative trading strategies based on a documented crash factor. One strategy is built on the crash
Page 1: factor and market timing (i.e., Crash + Timing Strategy, CTS), while the other is constructed by applying the crash factor and
Page 1: momentum-reversal strategy (i.e., Crash + Momentum-Reversal Strategy, CMRS). Our empirical analyses present stable returns and

Page 2: 2. Construction of trading strategies
Page 2: 2.1. Crash + timing strategy
Page 2: According to the concept of mean reversion and the gamblerΓÇÖs fallacy (Tversky and Kahneman, 1971), stock prices can rebound
Page 2: from the current crash and possibly experience another crash. Investors can generate profits by buying the dips during the crash and
Page 2: selling the rips during the consequent rebound. We use the crash factor2 of Jang and Kang (2019) to screen stocks. Specifically, top 10%
Page 2: stocks at the end of the previous month (t - 1) and the current month (t) are sorted out by the crash factor, and the top 10% stocks at the
Page 2: end of the current month are regarded as stocks whose bubbles just burst. However, stocks selected solely based on crash factor could
Page 2: again take a nosedive, so they cannot be directly put into quantitative investing.3 We consider appropriate timing for buying and
Page 2: selling (i.e. market timing) by constructing the crash + timing strategy.
Page 2: Due to the self-serving memory bias, investors generally repeat their previous behaviors for a risky stock position (Go╠êdker et al.,
Page 2: 2020). Therefore, we assume that before a stock market crashes, stocks will display characteristics similar to those perceived during the
Page 2: last crash. The strength/weakness of buying and selling on long and short positions before and after a crash would be the best indicator
Page 2: for market timing measurement. Consequently, we use the relative strength index (RSI) (Wilder, 1978) to identify the best times to buy
Page 2: stocks, and apply a momentum factor, which is defined as the corresponding 5-day momentum at the peak from last month, to capture
Page 2: the sell signals. During an observation period, we sell stocks when the momentum factor is similar to the peak momentum factor in the
Page 2: previous month (t-1). The specific process of CTS is given in Algorithm 1.
Page 2: 2.2. Crash + momentum-reversal strategy
Page 2: Momentum (Jegadeesh and Titman, 1993) and reversal are two well-known anomalies within the field of behavioral finance.
Page 2: However, momentum strategies suffer from occasional large crashes (i.e. momentum crash) (Barroso and Santa-Clara, 2015; Daniel
Page 2: and Moskowitz, 2016). During a momentum crash, momentum often tends to reverse. That is, the portfolio with the lowest momentum
Page 2: becomes the best performer, and the momentum-reversal strategy realizes excess returns. Therefore, the momentum-reversal strategy
Page 2: can considerably enhance investment efficiency.
Page 2: The second strategy is to combine the crash factor with momentum-reversal strategy. We first add the crash factor to the
Page 2: momentum-reversal strategy, adopt a scoring method to construct a new comprehensive scoring factor, and use this new factor to
Page 2: screen stocks. The stocks selected are featured with crashes in current month and expected to rebound later. That is, underperformed
Page 2: stocks will likely outperform in the future (Bali et al., 2011). The process of CMRS is shown in Algorithm 2.
Page 2: 3. Empirical analyses
Page 2: We perform empirical analyses using daily stock data from January 1, 2018 to December 31, 2018 and the Center for Research in
Page 2: Security Prices (CRSP) value-weighted index as the market index. The sample includes all stocks with share codes 10 and 11 from
Page 2: NYSE, AMEX and NASDAQ. All data are from the CRSP database. The data processing and calculation of crash factor are conducted
Page 2: based on Jang and Kang (2019) (See Appendix 1).
Page 2: 3.1. Performance of CTS
Page 2: To determine when to buy stocks, we choose the 14-day RSI4 within the oversold level as the buy limit price. An RSI below 30
Page 2: (Wilder, 1978) would suggest a buy. Since a rebound may happen or continue beyond the current month, we use the next two months
Page 2: as the observation period. In addition, we consider the following four methods to determine the sell threshold.
Page 2: Method 1: Use RSI as the benchmark and the overbought level (RSI=70) as a threshold;
Page 2: Method 2: Adopt the pre-set take profit level (i.e., 3%)5 as a threshold;

Page 2: Method 4: Consider the highest cumulative return over the last month captured by the momentum-information discreteness (Da
Page 2: et al., 2014) as a threshold.
Page 2: When adopting different methods to identify the best times to sell stocks, the stop-loss order is executed at 2.5% below the current
Page 2: stock price (Han et al., 2016). Evaluating the effectiveness of a strategy is essential for an investment. We evaluate our strategies based

Page 4: 3.2. Performance of CMRS
Page 4: In order to evaluate the performance of CMRS, all momentum factors and crash factors at the end of the month (t) are sorted in
Page 4: ascending and descending order, respectively, and the comprehensive scoring factors are calculated according to the scoring method.7
Page 4: The comprehensive scoring factors are sorted in descending order, and divided into five groups (Fama and French, 1993). We buy the
Page 4: stocks in these five groups in month t and sell them at the end of the month (t + 1). Finally, we calculate the cumulative return in each
Page 4: group. The results are shown in Fig. 1, where each curve represents the cumulative log return of a quintile regression group.
Page 4: Despite of some fluctuations in the market of 2018, the cumulative return of each group shows a clear monotonic pattern. Among
Page 4: them, the cumulative returns of quintiles 3 to 5 are all higher than the cumulative return of the market index, which indicates that the
Page 4: trading strategy brings investors an excess return. We also compare and analyze the performance of both CMRS and the traditional
Page 4: momentum-reversal strategy by using four indicators, i.e., annualized return, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown and win rate. The
Page 4: results are presented in Table 2.
Page 4: Both strategies show strong portfolio performance. All indicators in quintiles 1ΓÇô5 show clear monotonic patterns. CMRS out┬¡
Page 4: performs the momentum-reversal strategy in quintiles 1ΓÇô3. CMRS also outperforms the market index in quintiles 3ΓÇô5. Although the
Page 4: best performer Quintile 5 of CMRS falls behind the control group of the momentum-reversal strategy, this trading strategy is overall an
Page 4: effective stock selection strategy.
Page 4: Generally speaking, momentum crash is the most important factor that restricts application of the momentum strategy, and it can
Page 4: sometimes cause the momentum factor to fail. Although the market fluctuated in 2018, there was no momentum crashes. That is the
Page 4: main reason that we cannot find an obvious advantage of our proposed strategy in Table 2. To show that CMRS is effective during a
Page 4: momentum crash, we examine the data from January 1, 2015 to December 31, 2015 (momentum crashes happened over this period) to
Page 4: test the robustness of our trading strategies. The results show that both CTS and CMRS achieve favorable results, but the momentumreversal strategy generally fails. Please see Appendix 2 for details.
Page 4: In addition, we examine an up market period from January 1, 2019 to July 31, 2019 to test whether our trading strategies would
Page 4: work when the overall market return is positive. We find that the CTS strategy performs even better in the up market as annualized
Page 4: returns and win rates in 2019 are higher than those in 2018 and 2015. Results from the CMRS strategy are similar to those in 2018.
Page 4: Those results are not tabulated, but they are available on request.
Page 4: 4. Conclusion
Page 4: Inspired by the latest studies on stock market crashes, this paper constructs two quantitative trading strategies and tests their
Page 4: performances. Empirical analyses show that 1) the crash factor cannot directly function as a momentum factor, but when the crash
Page 4: factor combines with an appropriate timing indicator, it could generate excess returns for investors; 2) the momentum factor is useful
Page 4: in a market timing approach; 3) during a volatile market, a momentum-reversal strategy may fail while the CMRS proposed in this
Page 4: paper is still effective.
Page 4: Unlike previous studies, this article explores quantitative trading strategies from the perspective of behavioral finance, considering
Page 4: 7
Page 4: The so-called scoring method is a method where stocksΓÇÖ crash factors are ranked in ascending order, their momentum factors are ranked in
Page 4: descending order, and the average of the two ranks is defined as the comprehensive scoring factor.
