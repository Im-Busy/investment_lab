## Understanding Patterns and Their Limits

### Defining Patterns

- A pattern is bounded by at least two trend lines (straight or curved)
- All patterns have a combination of entry and exit points
- Patterns can be **continuation patterns** or **reversal patterns**
- Patterns are **fractal**, meaning that they can be seen in any charting period (weekly, daily, minute, etc.)
- A pattern is **not complete or activated until an actual breakout occurs**

### The Limits of Patterns ⚠️

> **Keep in Mind:** Some of our human tendencies can be dangerous for investors.
> - See patterns where there aren't any
> - Believe "market lore," technical and fundamental, without evidence
> - Look backwards rather than forward
> - Stick with original price targets of patterns after conditions have changed

## Techniques for Trading Patterns

### Core Techniques
- **Breakouts**
- **Entry Stops**
- **Protective Stops**
- **Retracements**

### Breakouts
> Violation of Trend Line, Support or Resistance, or previous reversal point. It signifies that a change in buyer and seller behavior and signals the beginning or end of a trend.

#### 📊 Illustration: Resistance Breakout
*Visual Description:* A line chart showing price action approaching a horizontal resistance line. The price breaks above the resistance line, indicating a bullish breakout. The vertical axis represents Price; the horizontal axis represents Time.

### Confirmation Filters

**Types of Filters:**
- Intrabar
- Multiple closes
- Time
- Percentage or point
- Money

> Apply a confirmation filter to determine whether a breakout has taken place.

### Entry Stops

> Buy stop orders are used to enter trades once the price breaks out.

#### 📊 Illustration: Entry Stop Examples
*Visual Description:* Two diagrams side-by-side:
1. **Resistance Breakout**: Price breaks above a horizontal resistance line; a buy stop order is placed just above the breakout level.
2. **Trend Line Breakout**: Price breaks above a downward-sloping trend line; a buy stop is placed above the breakout point.

### False and Failed Breakouts

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **False Breakout** | Price breaks out but almost immediately returns back through its breakout price. |
| **Failed Breakout (Trap)** | False breakout occurs and the price then breaks out in the opposite direction. |

#### 📊 Illustration: False vs. Failed Breakout
*Visual Description:* A multi-panel chart showing:
- **Left panel (False Breakout)**: Price briefly pierces a horizontal resistance line, then falls back below it, returning through the breakout level. A trend line is shown below.
- **Right panels (Failed Breakout/Trap)**: After a false breakout above resistance, price reverses sharply downward, breaking below support—trapping bullish traders.

### Protective Stops

> **Purpose:** Protects capital; determines the amount of capital risk before entry.

**Types of Placement:**
- Filters, such as percent, points, or money
- Trend line, support or resistance level with filter

#### 📊 Illustration: Protective Stop Placement
*Visual Description:* A chart showing price breaking above a resistance line. A horizontal dashed line labeled "Protective Stop" is placed below the breakout bar (or below a support level with a filter buffer), indicating where a stop-loss order would be set to limit downside risk.

### Trading False Breakouts Using Protective Stops

**Example Strategy:**
1. Enter on breakout
2. Place protective stop outside breakout bar opposite from breakout direction
3. Place entry stop at same level (called a "stop and reverse" order)
4. If price continues in direction of breakout → profit from breakout entry
5. If breakout is false → profit from stop and reverse

#### 📊 Illustration: Stop-and-Reverse Strategy
*Visual Description:* A candlestick chart showing:
- An initial breakout above resistance
- A "Protective sell stop and short entry stop" placed below the breakout bar
- Two potential outcomes:
  - **Success path**: Price continues upward → long position profits
  - **Failure path**: Price reverses downward through the protective stop → short position is triggered, profiting from the reversal

### Retracements

> **Counter Trend Correction**

**Types:**
- **Pullback** (on breakout down)
- **Throwback** (on breakout up)

**Waiting for Retracements:**
- Don't always occur
- Performance can suffer when they do

#### 📊 Illustration: Retracement (Pullback)
*Visual Description:* A chart showing price breaking below a trend line (downward breakout), then briefly retracing upward toward the broken trend line (now acting as resistance) before continuing downward. The retracement is labeled "Pullback."

### Horizontal Congestion: Double Top

**Characteristics:**
- Two successive peaks separated by an opposite reversal point
- Either rounded or pointed peaks that are usually at roughly the same price (resistance level)
- Price must break out of middle reversal point

#### 📊 Illustration: Double Top (Breakout Down)
*Visual Description:* A line chart showing:
- Two distinct peaks at approximately the same price level, forming a resistance line
- A trough between them forming a support line (the "neckline")
- Price breaks **below** the support line → bearish signal
- A "Pullback Entry" point is shown where price briefly retraces to the broken support (now resistance) before continuing down
- **Target Calculation**: Take the height from the highest peak to the trough, then subtract that amount from the breakout price to generate a downside price target.

---

### Horizontal Congestion: Double Bottom

**Characteristics:**
- Two successive troughs separated by a peak
- Either rounded or pointed troughs that are usually at roughly the same price (support level)
- Price must break out of middle peak

#### 📊 Illustration: Double Bottom (Breakout Up)
*Visual Description:* A line chart showing:
- Two distinct troughs at approximately the same price level, forming a support line
- A peak between them forming a resistance line
- Price breaks **above** the resistance line → bullish signal
- A "Throwback Entry" point is shown where price briefly retraces to the broken resistance (now support) before continuing up
- **Target Calculation**: Take the distance from the troughs to the peak, then add that amount to the breakout price to generate an upside price target.

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

---

### Horizontal Congestion: Triple Top

**Characteristics:**
- Three distinct peaks at roughly the same price level separated by two intermittent troughs
- Breakout occurs when price exceeds the extreme of the intermittent trough or a trend line connecting those points

#### 📊 Illustration: Triple Top (Breakout Down)
*Visual Description:* A line chart showing:
- Three peaks aligned horizontally at a resistance level
- Two troughs between them forming a support line
- Price breaks **below** the support line → bearish confirmation
- A "Pullback" entry opportunity is marked where price retests the broken support
- **Target Calculation**: Take the height from the highest peak to the lowest trough in the pattern, then subtract that amount from the lowest trough to generate a downside price target.

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Horizontal Congestion: Triple Bottom

**Characteristics:**
- Three distinct troughs at roughly the same price level separated by two intermittent peaks at any level
- Breakout occurs when price exceeds the extreme of the intermittent peaks or a trend line connecting those points
- Best performance may be after a sustained decline*
- Average performance, but watch for failures*

#### 📊 Illustration: Triple Bottom (Breakout Up)
*Visual Description:* A line chart showing:
- Three troughs aligned horizontally at a support level
- Two peaks between them forming a resistance line
- Price breaks **above** the resistance line → bullish confirmation
- A "Throwback" entry opportunity is marked where price retests the broken resistance
- **Target Calculation**: Take the height from the highest peak to the lowest trough in the pattern, then add that amount to the highest peak to generate an upside price target.

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Horizontal Congestion: Rectangles

**Characteristics:**
- Trading range with support and resistance levels bounding price action
- Slight tilt, similar to horizontal channel
- Often has many false breakouts*
- **Things to consider:**
  - Confirm a breakout
  - "Shortfall" often indicator of eventual breakout direction
- Best occurrence may be bottom breaking upward*

#### 📊 Illustration: Rectangle (Entry Up, Breakout Up)
*Visual Description:* A chart showing:
- Price oscillating between a horizontal resistance line (top) and support line (bottom), forming a rectangular consolidation zone
- Price breaks **above** resistance → bullish signal
- A "Throwback Entry" is shown where price retraces to the broken resistance (now support)
- A "Breakout Shortfall" annotation shows price failing to break support, hinting at eventual upside breakout
- **Target Calculation**: Take the height from resistance to support. For upside breakout: add to resistance line. For downside breakout: subtract from support line.

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

---

### Triangle: Symmetrical

**Characteristics:**
- Bounded by a downward sloping upper trend line and an upward sloping lower trend line. Each bound is a straight trend line
- Prices must touch each bound at least twice. Many false breakouts.
- Moderately successful in performance
- **Things to consider:** Confirm a breakout
- Best occurrence may be upward breaking out – above average for all patterns*

#### 📊 Illustration: Symmetrical Triangle (Breakout Up)
*Visual Description:* A chart showing:
- Converging trend lines: upper line slopes down, lower line slopes up, forming a symmetrical triangle
- Price consolidates within the triangle, touching each boundary multiple times
- Price breaks **above** the upper trend line → bullish signal
- A "Throwback" entry is shown where price retests the broken upper trend line (now support)
- A "Target Line" is drawn vertically from the breakout point, with distance equal to the triangle's height at its widest point
- A "Cradle" annotation marks the initial consolidation area before the triangle forms
- **Target Calculation**: Take the height from the highest peak to the lowest trough in the pattern. Add to breakout price for upward breakouts; subtract for downward breakouts.

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

---

### Triangle: Ascending

**Characteristics:**
- Bounded by a horizontal upper trend line and an upward sloping lower trend line. Each bound is a straight trend line
- Prices can break in either direction, but more commonly upward*
- Breakout usually occurs in pattern.
- About average failure rates but many small false breakouts*
- Post breakout performance average on upside but above average on downside*

#### 📊 Illustration: Ascending Triangle (Breakout Down)
*Visual Description:* A chart showing:
- Horizontal resistance line at the top; upward-sloping support line at the bottom, forming an ascending triangle
- Price makes higher lows while testing the same resistance level
- Price breaks **below** the support line → bearish signal (less common but possible)
- A "Pullback Entry" is shown where price retraces to the broken support (now resistance)
- A "Target Line" projects the downside move equal to the triangle's height
- **Target Calculation**: Same as symmetrical triangle—use pattern height added/subtracted from breakout price.

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

---

### Triangle: Descending

**Characteristics:**
- Bounded by two trend lines; the lower is horizontal and the upper slopes downward
- Prices can break in either direction but most commonly downward*
- Above-average performance on upside break; retracements occur often*

#### 📊 Illustration: Descending Triangle (Breakout Down)
*Visual Description:* A chart showing:
- Downward-sloping resistance line at the top; horizontal support line at the bottom, forming a descending triangle
- Price makes lower highs while testing the same support level
- Price breaks **below** the horizontal support → bearish signal
- A "Pullback Entry" is shown where price retraces to the broken support (now resistance)
- A "Target Line" projects the downside move
- **Target Calculation**: Same methodology—pattern height added/subtracted from breakout price based on direction.

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

---

### Triangle: Wedge

**Characteristics:**
- Bounded by two trend lines, each headed in the **same direction**; Price must touch a trend line at least five times (3 times on one and 2 times on the other) before a breakout
- Often occur following a panic (declining wedge) or bubble (rising wedge)
- Performance in both types is below average, and retracements are very common*

#### 📊 Illustration: Rising Wedge (Breakout Down from Climax Peak)
*Visual Description:* A chart showing:
- Two upward-sloping converging trend lines (both rising, but upper line rises more steeply), forming a rising wedge
- Price oscillates within the wedge, touching boundaries multiple times
- A "Climax Peak" marks the final high before breakdown
- Price breaks **below** the lower trend line → bearish reversal signal
- A "Pullback" entry is shown where price retests the broken lower trend line
- **Target Calculation**:
  - For downward breakout: the lowest trough in the pattern is the price target
  - For upward breakout: add pattern height (highest peak to lowest trough) to breakout price

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Head and Shoulders: Top

**Characteristics:**
- Three peaks with center peak higher than the other two
- Shoulders should be at approximately the same level and the head higher
- Line connecting the two troughs between the peaks is called the **"neckline"**
- Pattern is only complete on breaking the neckline
- Target is the distance from the head to the neckline projected from the neckline
- This is a standard pattern for tops and has one of the lowest failure rates

#### 📊 Illustration: Head and Shoulders Top
*Visual Description:* A line chart showing:
- **Left Shoulder**: First peak
- **Head**: Central, highest peak
- **Right Shoulder**: Third peak, roughly equal in height to left shoulder
- **Neckline**: A trend line connecting the two troughs between the shoulders and head (can be horizontal or sloped)
- Price breaks **below** the neckline → bearish confirmation
- A "Target" line is drawn downward from the neckline breakout point, with distance equal to the vertical distance from the head peak to the neckline
- Entry is typically on neckline breakout or on a pullback to the neckline (now resistance)

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

---

### Head and Shoulders: Bottom (Inverse)

**Characteristics:**
- Inverted but otherwise identical to a top pattern except not as profitable*

#### 📊 Illustration: Head and Shoulders Bottom (Breakout Up)
*Visual Description:* A line chart showing:
- **Left Shoulder**: First trough
- **Head**: Central, lowest trough
- **Right Shoulder**: Third trough, roughly equal in depth to left shoulder
- **Neckline**: Trend line connecting the two peaks between the troughs
- Price breaks **above** the neckline → bullish confirmation
- A "Throwback" entry is shown where price retraces to the broken neckline (now support)
- Target projection: distance from head to neckline, added to neckline breakout point for upside target
- Note: Performance is generally weaker than the top pattern

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Cup and Handle (also "Saucer")

**Characteristics:**
- Pattern consists of a rounded bottom (not a "V" bottom), two "lips" at each end, and a "handle" (similar to a flag pattern) from the handle
- Pattern is complete with breakout above both lips
- Often have a throwback
- The pattern's performance ranks about average for bottom patterns*

#### 📊 Illustration: Cup and Handle (Breakout Up)
*Visual Description:* A chart showing:
- **Cup/Bowl**: A broad, U-shaped rounded bottom (not sharp V), with "Lip" annotations at both ends at approximately equal price levels
- **Handle**: A small downward-sloping consolidation (like a flag) forming on the right side after the right lip
- Price breaks **above** the handle resistance and the right lip → bullish confirmation
- A "Throwback" entry is shown where price retraces to the breakout level
- A "Breakout Down" annotation on the left shows a prior false breakdown that was reversed
- **Target Calculation**: Take the height from the right cup lip to the bottom of the cup, then add that amount to the breakout price.

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

## Candlestick Patterns: General Information

**Characteristics:**
- Traditionally, candlestick patterns are **reversal patterns**, meaning they are used to identify when a trend is ending
- Candlestick patterns are often used with longer-term trends; thus:
  - Upward reversal patterns after a correction in a longer upward trend show the best performance
  - Vice versa for downward reversal patterns in a long downward trend
- ⚠️ Be careful not to act on a perceived candle pattern until the pattern has formed and is activated by a breakout in a certain direction

### 🕯️ Candlestick Anatomy Reference
*Visual Description:* A labeled candlestick diagram showing:
- **Body**: Rectangle between open and close prices (white/green = close > open; black/red = close < open)
- **Upper wick/shadow**: Line from top of body to high price
- **Lower wick/shadow**: Line from bottom of body to low price
- Annotations for Open, Close, High, Low prices

### Doji

**Characteristics:**
- A one-candle pattern formed when the open and close are the same price, and the high and low are roughly equidistant from the open and close
- Extremely common
- Indicates indecision in the marketplace and thus is a possible warning of price change

#### 📊 Illustration: Doji Candle
*Visual Description:* A single candlestick with:
- A very small or nonexistent body (open ≈ close), appearing as a horizontal line or tiny cross
- Upper and lower wicks of approximately equal length extending above and below
- Labeled "Doji"; may appear at tops or bottoms of trends as a potential reversal warning

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

---

### Harami

**Characteristics:**
- A two-candle pattern of a large body of either color followed by a small body of the opposite color; The second body is completely within the body of the large body and is called a "spinning top"
- Although common belief is that the harami is a reversal pattern, many report that it has the potential of breaking either way
- A variation that has a doji instead of a spinning top as the second candle has equally average performance and random breakout*

#### 📊 Illustration: Harami Pattern
*Visual Description:* Two candlesticks side-by-side:
- **First candle**: Large body (black or white), representing strong momentum
- **Second candle**: Small body ("spinning top") of opposite color, entirely contained within the vertical range of the first candle's body
- Labels: "Top" and "Bottom" indicate price extremes; note that candle body color can be black or white
- Two variants shown: bullish harami (large black → small white) and bearish harami (large white → small black)

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Hanging Man and Hammer

**Characteristics:**
- One-candle patterns differentiated by the color of the body. Each pattern has a high that coincides with either the opening or closing price
- **Hanging Man**: Thought to be a continuation pattern, actually breaks in either direction randomly with a slight upward bias. Its overall performance is below average*
- **Hammer**: Occurs relatively frequently but has below-average performance*

#### 📊 Illustration: Hanging Man vs. Hammer
*Visual Description:* Two identical-shaped candlesticks labeled differently based on context:
- Small body at the top of the candle's range
- Long lower wick (at least 2x the body length)
- Little or no upper wick
- **Hanging Man**: Appears after an uptrend → potential bearish reversal (body can be black or white)
- **Hammer**: Appears after a downtrend → potential bullish reversal (body can be black or white)
- Labels: "Top" and "Bottom" indicate price extremes

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Shooting Star and Inverted Hammer

**Characteristics:**
- A one-candle inverted hanging man or hammer pattern. Hammers by themselves have white bodies and shooting stars have black bodies
- As a one-candle pattern, the shooting star has average performance. The same is true for the single inverted hammer*

#### 📊 Illustration: Shooting Star vs. Inverted Hammer
*Visual Description:* Two candlesticks with:
- Small body at the bottom of the candle's range
- Long upper wick (at least 2x the body length)
- Little or no lower wick
- **Shooting Star**: Appears after an uptrend → potential bearish reversal (typically black body)
- **Inverted Hammer**: Appears after a downtrend → potential bullish reversal (typically white body)
- Note: "Either Can Be Black or White" annotation indicates color is less critical than structure and context
- Labels: "Top" and "Bottom" indicate price extremes

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Engulfing

**Characteristics:**
- A two-bar pattern in which the second bar body completely engulfs the first bar body*
- A bottom engulfing pattern, with a short black body followed by a tall white body, is thought to be an upward reversal pattern and actually has very good performance on a downward breakout in a downward trend*

#### 📊 Illustration: Engulfing Pattern
*Visual Description:* Two candlesticks:
- **First candle**: Small body (black or white)
- **Second candle**: Large body of opposite color that completely "engulfs" the first candle's body (open and close of second candle are outside the open/close of the first)
- **Bullish Engulfing**: Small black → large white (after downtrend)
- **Bearish Engulfing**: Small white → large black (after uptrend)
- Labels: "Top" and "Bottom" indicate price extremes

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Dark Cloud Cover and Piercing Line

**Characteristics:**
- **Dark Cloud Cover**: Two-bar pattern where the second bar opens higher than the first, gaps up, then closes lower—more than halfway into the first bar's body—and is black versus white in the first bar
- **Piercing Line**: Opposite of dark cloud cover; second bar is white, gaps down, then closes higher—more than halfway into the first bar's black body
- The dark cloud is thought to be a downward reversing pattern
- The piercing line pattern is thought to be an upward reversing pattern*

#### 📊 Illustration: Dark Cloud Cover & Piercing Line
*Visual Description:* Two side-by-side two-candle patterns:
- **Dark Cloud Cover (Bearish)**:
  1. First candle: Long white (bullish) body
  2. Second candle: Opens above first candle's high (gap up), then closes below the midpoint of the first candle's body, forming a black (bearish) candle
- **Piercing Line (Bullish)**:
  1. First candle: Long black (bearish) body
  2. Second candle: Opens below first candle's low (gap down), then closes above the midpoint of the first candle's body, forming a white (bullish) candle
- Labels: "Top" and "Bottom" indicate price extremes

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Pennant/Flag

**Characteristics:**
- Pennant and flag patterns are variations of the same pattern
- These patterns are often preceded by a steep, sharp price change, up or down, and form a short consolidation that appears like a triangle or flag
- Generally, the pattern slopes slightly in the direction opposite from the trend
- The breakout in either direction is often followed by a move that equals the earlier steep, sharp price change into the pattern

#### 📊 Illustration: Flag and Pennant in Upward Trend
*Visual Description:* A chart showing:
- **Flag Pole**: A steep, near-vertical price surge
- **Flag**: A small, downward-sloping rectangular consolidation channel after the pole
- **Pennant**: A small symmetrical triangle consolidation after the pole (alternative to flag)
- Price breaks **above** the flag/pennant → continuation of uptrend
- **Target Calculation**: Take the height from the start of the "flag pole" to the highest peak in the pennant/flag. Add that amount to the breakout point for an upward price target (measured move).
- Annotations distinguish "Flag" (parallel channels) vs. "Pennant" (converging trend lines)

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Gaps

#### 📊 Illustration: Gap Up
*Visual Description:* A bar/candlestick chart showing:
- A clear vertical "gap" between the prior day's high/close and the current day's low/open
- No price trading occurred in the gapped zone
- Labeled "Gap Up" with arrows indicating the price jump
- Context: Gaps are caused by appreciable changes in supply and demand from one close to the following open

**Characteristics:**
- Definition: No trading (gap) at specific prices
- Gaps can be considered "up" or "down"
- Gaps are caused by appreciable changes in supply and demand from one close to the following open

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Trading Gaps: Explosion Gap Pivot Strategy

**Characteristics:**
- Gaps are generally profitable on breakouts from patterns, trends, support or resistance
- A method of trading a gap is the "explosion gap pivot." It assures that the gap is valid
- After the gap, wait for "throwback." If throwback "covers" the gap (fills it), no action. If the throwback stops, this is called the "pivot low." Place buy entry above high of the gap bar
- "Pivot" is the lowest level of the post-gap breakout
- Protective stops initially placed at gap low and then below pivot low

#### 📊 Illustration: Gap Trading with Pivot Low
*Visual Description:* A candlestick chart showing:
1. A large upward gap (empty space between candles)
2. Price pulls back after the gap but does not fill it completely
3. The lowest point of the pullback is labeled "Pivot Low"
4. A buy entry order is placed above the high of the gap candle
5. A protective stop is initially at the gap's low, then moved below the pivot low
6. Price then resumes upward, confirming the breakout
- Annotations: "Gap," "Pivot Low," "Breakout," and entry/stop levels clearly marked

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Two-Bar Reversal Button or Pipe Bottom

**Characteristics:**
- Two bars and occurs at the end of a large trend, up or down trend. Ideally, the first bar, in a bottom pattern, closes at the low, and the second bar closes in the upper half of the range.
- It is more reliable in weekly data
- Bar ranges are larger than preceding bar ranges
- Action occurs on breakout through second bar

#### 📊 Illustration: Two-Day Reversal Bottom (Pipe Bottom)
*Visual Description:* Two consecutive candlesticks at a trend low:
- **First bar**: Long black candle closing near its low (strong selling)
- **Second bar**: Long white candle opening near prior low but closing in upper half of its range (strong buying reversal)
- Combined shape resembles a "U" or pipe bottom
- Breakout confirmed when price moves above the high of the second bar
- **Target Calculation**: Take the height from the taller of the two bars to the lower of the two bars. Add that amount to the taller bar's high to get a price target.

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Volatility Patterns: Inside Bar

**Characteristics:**
- Dull activity is known as "low volatility." New trends often begin from periods of low volatility
- One way to look at volatility is to observe the relationship between price bars
- "Range" is the spread between high and low in a price bar
- If a bar is followed by a bar with less range, volatility is declining; the second bar is called a "narrow range" bar
- When this second bar's range is contained within the range of its preceding bar, it is called an "inside bar."

#### 📊 Illustration: Inside Bar Setup
*Visual Description:* Two candlesticks:
- **First bar (Mother bar)**: Large range candle
- **Second bar (Inside bar)**: Smaller candle whose high and low are entirely within the high/low range of the mother bar
- Trading signals:
  - Buy next bar at opening above inside bar close
  - Sell next bar at opening below inside bar close
- Annotations highlight the containment relationship

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*

### Narrow Range (NR4)

**Characteristics:**
- One low volatility pattern is called a "Narrow Range" pattern and consists of a bar with a range narrower than its preceding bars
- The graph shows a four-bar, Narrow Range pattern (NR4) with four bars, the fourth bar having a narrower range than the preceding three bars
- The breakout occurs on a break above or below the high or low of the narrow range

#### 📊 Illustration: NR4 Pattern
*Visual Description:* Four consecutive candlesticks:
- Bars 1–3: Progressively or variably sized ranges
- Bar 4: Noticeably narrower range than bars 1–3 (labeled "Range on day 4 less than days 1–3")
- Breakout signals:
  - **Buy**: Price breaks above the high of bar 4
  - **Sell**: Price breaks below the low of bar 4
- Annotation: "NR4" labels the four-bar pattern

> *Source: Technical Analysis: The Complete Resource for Financial Market Technicians, 2nd ed.*
