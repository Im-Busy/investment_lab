## Chapter 1: Butterfly Pattern Trading Strategy

### What is the Butterfly Harmonic Pattern?

In technical analysis, the Butterfly harmonic pattern is a **reversal pattern composed of four legs**. A leg is a swing wave movement that connects and is composed of a swing high and a swing low.

### Butterfly Harmonic Pattern Rules Summary:
1. **AB** = 0.786 or 78.6% retracement of the XA swing leg
2. **BC** = minimum 38.2% – maximum 88.6% of AB swing leg
3. **CD** = minimum 1.618 – maximum 2.618 of AB swing leg
4. **CD** = minimum 1.272% – maximum 1.618% of XA swing leg

> **📊 Illustration Description: Defining the Butterfly Harmonic Pattern**
> *A line chart diagram showing the Butterfly pattern structure with five labeled points: X, A, B, C, and D. The pattern starts at point X, moves to A (impulsive leg), retraces to B (at 78.6% of XA), extends to C (between 38.2%-88.6% of AB), and finally completes at D (between 1.272-1.618 extension of XA). Fibonacci retracement and extension levels are marked with horizontal dashed lines. Arrows indicate the direction of each swing leg (XA, AB, BC, CD). The Potential Reversal Zone (PRZ) at point D is highlighted.*

### Defining the Butterfly Harmonic Pattern
The Butterfly harmonic pattern depends upon the **B point** as it defines the structure and sets up the other measurements within the pattern to define the trade opportunities. For the Butterfly pattern, the B point must possess a precise **78.6% retracement of the XA swing**.

Other rules that redefine the structure further include:
- The BC projection must be at least 1.618 measurements
- The Butterfly pattern must include an equivalent AB=CD pattern (minimum requirement); however, the alternate 1.27AB=CD is more common for this structure
- The **1.27XA projection** is the most critical number in the potential reversal zones
- The C point must be within the range of **0.382-0.886 retracement**

You can start drawing the butterfly pattern as soon as you have the first two legs of the pattern. Once you have the points X, A and B you can begin monitoring the price action and look for confirmation that wave C conforms the Butterfly pattern rules.

### What is the Cypher Pattern Forex?

In the harmonic pattern world, the Cypher pattern forex is a **four-leg reversal pattern** that follows specific Fibonacci ratios. The Cypher pattern forex appears less frequent than other harmonic patterns because it's hard for the market price to satisfy such rigid Fibonacci ratios.

### Cypher Pattern Fibonacci Rules:
- **AB** = 0.382 to 0.618 retracement of the XA swing leg
- **BC** = extend to minimum 1.272 and maximum 1.414 of the XA swing leg
- **CD** = retrace to 0.786 of the XC swing leg

> **📊 Illustration Description: Defining the Cypher Pattern Structure**
> *A diagram showing the Cypher pattern with points X, A, B, C, D. Key features: (1) AB retraces to 0.382-0.618 of XA; (2) BC extends beyond X to 1.272-1.414 of XA (point C is higher than X in bullish pattern); (3) CD retraces to 0.786 of the entire XC move. Fibonacci levels are labeled on each leg. The pattern resembles a "W" or "M" shape depending on bullish/bearish orientation. The entry zone at point D (0.786 XC) is highlighted.*

### Defining the Strategy
1. The retracement from X up to A has to come down and touch the **0.382 Fibonacci ratios** but can't close below the **0.618 Fibonacci ratios**. Between 0.382 and 0.618 Fibonacci retracements of XA swing-leg, we have point **"B"**.
2. The next rule: BC is a Fibonacci extension of the XA leg that comes in at **1.27** but doesn't exceed **1.414**. This point is labeled **"C"** and completes the BC swing-leg.
3. The final leg (point D) is located at the **0.786 Fibonacci retracements** of the entire move started from X up to C. This is where orders are executed.

## Chapter 5: Crab Pattern Harmonic Trading Strategy

### Crab vs. Butterfly: Key Difference
The harmonic pattern Crab is another unique harmonic pattern which has a price structure that looks the same as the Butterfly chart pattern. **The difference is the CD swing leg which has a bigger extension**.

### How to Trade the Crab Chart Pattern

The Crab market strategy like any other harmonic pattern is a **four-leg reversal pattern** that follows specific Fibonacci ratios.

### Crab Pattern Fibonacci Rules:
- **AB** = retrace between **0.382 – 0.618** Fibonacci Retracement of XA leg
- **BC** = minimum 38.2% and maximum 88.6% Fibonacci retracement of AB leg
- **CD** = Target between **2.24 – 3.618 Fibonacci extension of AB leg** OR ideal target of **1.618 of XA leg**

> ⚠️ **Note**: The Fibonacci retracement and ratios are at the core of harmonic trading. Make sure the above rules are satisfied before you trade the Crab harmonic pattern.

> **📊 Illustration Description: Crab Pattern Structure – Extreme Extensions**

The Potential Reversal Zone (PRZ) at D is wide and highlighted. Pattern resembles Butterfly but with exaggerated CD leg. Entry zone at 2.24 AB extension marked.*

### Key Characteristics:
- The harmonic Crab is an **extreme harmonic pattern** – price action will typically be volatile as it enters the **Potential Reversal Zone (PRZ)**
- In a bullish Crab, typically the highest number in the harmonic support zone is the alternate ABCD component: either **1.24 or 1.618 AB=CD**
- In most cases, the Crab pattern will have a **1.618 alternate AB=CD** (we still allow 1.24 to define a valid structure, but 1.618 is most common)

### Crab Pattern Harmonic Trading Strategy

Trading the Crab harmonic pattern can be perceived as a very hard trade to take because of the price action – price moves quickly towards the pattern completion point (PRZ).

- **Trailing SL**: Once market moves in your favor, move SL to completion of D leg

That's the logical place to hide your stop loss because any break below will automatically invalidate the Fibonacci requirements for a Crab harmonic.

> ⚠️ **Shortcoming**: The Crab pattern requires using a wide stop loss, but it can be overcome by trailing your SL once the trade starts to move in your favor (move SL right below point D).

For this specific harmonic pattern, we're proposing a **multiple take profit strategy**. The Crab remains a very reliable pattern but we want to cash in some quick profits since this is one of the most volatile harmonic patterns.

**Rationale for TP1 at 0.618:**
- Derived from the golden ratio 1.618 – one of the most important numbers in mathematics

## Chapter 6: Swimming with the Sharks – Shark Harmonic Trading Strategy

Retail traders are always going to be a small fish in the shark infested ocean. There are more powerful traders in the market and if you want to learn swimming with the sharks we present the **Shark Harmonic trading strategy**.

### Historical Context
The Shark pattern may be a relatively new harmonic pattern. It was discovered only recently in **2011 by Scott Carney** but it has the same features like all other harmonic patterns because it follows certain Fibonacci ratios for its structure to be validated.

### Shark vs. Crab: Similarities
The Harmonic Shark Pattern has some similarities with the Crab harmonic pattern because both of these harmonic patterns have an **overextended point C**.

### Time Frame Recommendation
The bullish Shark pattern can be traded on all time frames but we recommend only to trade harmonic chart patterns from the **1 hour time frame and above**.

### How to Trade the Shark Harmonic Pattern

The Shark trading strategy like any other harmonic pattern is a **five-leg reversal pattern** that follows specific Fibonacci ratios.

### Key Differentiators:
- **Five-point setup**: Labeled as **O, X, A, B, C** (unlike 4-point patterns)
- **Point B termination**: Ends **above wave X** and extends to minimum **1.13** and maximum **1.618** Fibonacci ratios

### Shark Pattern Fibonacci Rules:
- **AB** = retrace between **1.13 – 1.618 Fibonacci Extension** of XA leg *(extension, not retracement)*
- **BC** = extends to **113% Fibonacci extension** of 0X leg
- **CD** = Target of **50% Fibonacci Retracement** of BC leg

> ⚠️ **Note**: The Fibonacci retracement and ratios are at the core of harmonic trading. Make sure the above rules are satisfied before you trade the Shark harmonic pattern.

> **📊 Illustration Description: Shark Pattern Structure – 5-Point Setup**
> *Diagram showing Bullish Shark with points O-X-A-B-C-D. Unique features: (1) Starts at O (not X); (2) AB extends XA by 1.13-1.618 (B above X); (3) BC extends 0X by 113%; (4) CD retraces BC to 50%. Pattern resembles complex "W" shape. Entry at point C (not D) highlighted. D point marked as predefined profit target at 50% BC retracement.*

### Trading Approach:
- Unlike other harmonic patterns, **all trades are taken based on point C** while point D is actually used as a pre-defined profit target
- Ideally, completion point of CD swing-leg terminates at **50% Fibonacci retracement of BC leg** and also must satisfy the **AB=CD condition**
- Most common approach: Try to capture the last move of a complex pattern, entering at **C** with protective stop loss above/below the **2.24 of AB retracement** and targeting the **50% retracement of the BC swing-leg**

---

### Shark Harmonic Trading Strategy

The way we're proposing to trade this pattern is to **enter at the open of the next candle after our Harmonic indicator has spotted the Shark pattern**.

> 💡 **Clarification**: As soon as the C-leg is established, we enter at the market with a protective stop loss at the **2.618 extension of AB swing-leg**.

**Step-by-Step Guide:**
1. Click harmonic pattern indicator (TradingView right toolbar)
2. Identify starting point **O** (any swing high/low) – *Note: Shark starts at O, not X*
3. Follow market swing wave movements
4. Validate all points against Shark pattern Fibonacci ratios

#### Step #2: Entry – Buy at Point D (CD = 1.13 of OX leg) *[Bullish Example]*

The D to X retracement can be anywhere between **0.886 – 1.13**. But we prefer taking trades using the ideal **1.13 extension**.

> 🔍 The Shark pattern has some common characteristics with the Crab pattern because the wave D is a sharp price movement in both harmonic patterns.

#### Step #3: Stop Loss – Place Protective Stop Loss below the 1.150 Fibonacci extension of XA

- **Initial SL**: At 1.150 Fibonacci extension of XA
- **Trailing SL**: Once market starts moving toward first take profit price, move SL to completion of D leg

That's the logical place to hide your stop loss because any break below will automatically invalidate the Fibonacci requirements for a Shark pattern.

> ✅ **Biggest Advantage**: Trading the Shark pattern requires the use of a very tight stop loss. This is good news as we always want to minimize losses and maximize profits.

#### Step #4: Take Profit – Multiple Take Profit Strategy
- **TP1**: 50% CD Fibonacci retracement
- **TP2**: C swing high (100% retracement)

Because of the complexity and the price structure of the Shark pattern we're going to have two taking profit zones:
- **First TP zone**: 50% Fibonacci retracement of CD swing-leg
- **Second TP zone**: 100% retracement (C swing high)

> 🔑 **Rationale**: In essence the Shark pattern leads to the 5-0 pattern and once we reach the D point it can reverse. However, the Shark pattern can be a reversal pattern in itself and that's why we're using as a second target the 100% Fibonacci ratio.
