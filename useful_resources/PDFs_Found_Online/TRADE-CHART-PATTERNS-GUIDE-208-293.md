## 11.1 Adam-Eve Patterns

### Overview
"Adam and Eve" patterns are variations of "double tops" and "double bottoms" patterns. They have slightly more complex trading rules than regular "double bottom" and "double top" patterns, but consist of pattern trading rules similar to Triangles, Pennants and Wedges trading structures. Adam-Eve patterns are very reliable as they form at the market tops and at the market bottoms. Trading these patterns is relatively easy with low-risk trading rules.

### Pattern Characteristics
- **Adam part**: Looks like a sharp spike or "V" bottom
- **Eve part**: Has a round bottom (or top)
- **Rare sequences**: "Adam-Eve-Adam" or "Eve-Adam-Eve" patterns do occur and may be more reliable than "Adam-Eve" or "Eve-Adam" pairs. These patterns tend to signal much bigger moves.
- **Common sequences**: "Adam-Adam" or "Eve-Eve" patterns are also very common.

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade Entry** | Although visually easy to detect, confirmation is needed. Trades are entered in the direction of the breakout/breakdown of the middle spike between "Adam and Eve" structures.                                    |
| **Target**      | Targets can be set at the previous swing high/swing low of the first swing in the "double top" or "double bottom." Subsequent targets would be set at the next "higher swing high" or the next "lower swing low." |
| **Stop Loss**   | Protect trades using the pattern high for "short" trades and pattern low for "long" trades.                                                                                                                       |

## Trader Vic's 2B Patterns

### Source Reference
"Principles of Professional Speculation" written by Victor Sperandeo (Trader Vic), analyzes one of the powerful top/bottom reversal techniques.

### Core Principle
> "In an uptrend, if prices penetrate the previous high, but fail to carry through and immediately drop below the previous high, the trend is apt to reverse." The converse is true for a downtrend.

### Pattern Characteristics
- Also called "spring"
- The 2B setup looks like a micro "M" pattern
- Signals trend reversal when prices stop making higher-highs in an uptrend

### 2B Pattern Rule
1. Prices make a new high or new low
2. They pull back for a healthy retracement
3. After retracement, the price tries to re-test the new high or new low
4. When this test fails and does not maintain prices above the new high/low, it signals a potential trend reversal
5. This setup is very powerful and signals the beginning of a correction

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade Entry** | Market attempts to test a recent new high or low, but does not hold prices above this range. Enter to sell the low of the bar trying to breakout, or buy the high of the bar trying to breakdown. |
| **Target**      | For 2B Buy setup: "swing low" prior to the new high. For 2B Sell setup: "swing high" prior to the new low.                                                                                        |
| **Stop Loss**   | Protect "long" trade by placing stop below recent low; protect "short" trade by placing stop above recent high.                                                                                   |

## Trader Vic's 1-2-3 Patterns

### Source Reference
"Principles of Professional Speculation" by Trader Vic highlights another great reversal pattern called "1-2-3," also called "Three Point Reversal" or "ABC Reversal."

### Theoretical Basis
Based on Dow Jones Theory—change of trend:
- Prices that are rising or falling must break a trend line
- In uptrend: prices stop making higher highs (for shorts)
- In downtrend: prices stop making lower lows (for longs)
- During uptrend: prices try to reach recent high but may fail to close above
- During downtrend: prices may try to reach recent low and may fail to close below

### 1-2-3 Pattern Criteria
1. A trend line "breakout" or "breakdown" from the current trend
2. A "test" and "failure" of the previous "high" or "low" after the trend line breakout/breakdown
3. The breakout or breakdown of a "swing high" or "swing low" prior to rule 2

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Trade Entry** | After trend line breakout/breakdown and re-test of swing low/swing high: In uptrend, sell signal when prices close below swing low. In downtrend, buy signal when prices close above swing high. |
| **Target**      | For 1-2-3 bearish setup: "swing low" prior to the "new high." For 1-2-3 bullish setup: "swing high" prior to the "new low."                                                                      |
| **Stop Loss**   | For bearish setup (Short): stop above "swing high." For bullish setup (Long): stop below "swing low."                                                                                            |

## Pipe Pattern

### Pattern Characteristics
- Forms at market tops or bottoms due to price exhaustion
- First "Pipe" is a spike in prices, followed by another spike of at least similar length in opposite direction
- Looks like two parallel lines at market extremes
- Must be very distinct compared to rest of bars and visually obvious
- Can form during intra-day rallies or sell-offs, but more reliable in daily and weekly charts
- Daily "Pipe" patterns: two bars showing opposite sentiment spanned by significant time
- Trades entered in opposite direction of current trend
- Rare but one of the best patterns to trade

### Trading Rules

| Element                  | Rule                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Confirmation & Entry** | Pipe Top: Prices must trade below low of "lowest low" of two pipes to enter short. Pipe Bottom: Prices must trade above "highest high" of two pipes to enter long. |
| **Stop Loss**            | Pipe Bottom: Stop below "lowest low" of pattern. Pipe Top: Stop above "highest high" of pattern.                                                                   |
| **Target**               | First target: Length of larger of the two Pipes from entry. Second target: Twice the length of largest Pipe from entry.                                            |

## M and W Patterns

### Overview
- Usually considered as M-Top and W-Bottom patterns with few differences
- M and W Patterns usually **continue** prior trends, whereas M-Top and W-Bottom patterns **terminate** trends
- Frequently form during congestion zones
- M patterns rarely form at market bottoms; W patterns rarely form at market tops
- Volume concept similar to counterparts "Double tops" and "Double bottoms"
- Closely related to "Triple tops/triple bottoms" and "Head and Shoulders/Inverse Head and Shoulders" patterns

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Trade Entry** | Trades initiated at breakdown of neckline/hump level. For W-Bottoms: breakout of neckline/hump level. Enter long above high of breakout bar at neckline; enter short below low of breakdown bar at neckline. |
| **Targets**     | Both patterns: targets set at pattern high for W-Pattern and pattern low for M-Pattern.                                                                                                                      |
| **Stop Loss**   | M-pattern: stop loss above pattern high. W-Pattern: stop loss below pattern low.                                                                                                                             |

## Round Top Pattern

### Pattern Characteristics
- Usually reversal patterns generated over long periods of time
- Indecisive markets move in slow, gradual changes near tops and bottoms
- Formed when prices trade in tight range making sporadic "lower highs" and "higher lows"
- Volume traded in this pattern is very low compared to prior trend
- Breakdowns from Round top patterns will be fast and sharp when price movement occurs
- High failure rate

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade Entry** | Draw trend line connecting Round top to previous swings. Close below trend line indicates trend change. Enter short below low of breakdown bar.       |
| **Target**      | First target: Depth of Round top pattern from trend line breakdown level. Second target: Near prior "swing lows" before pattern formation.            |
| **Stop Loss**   | For inclined trend line breakdowns: stop above trend line (on close basis). For horizontal trend line breakdowns: use mid-point of Round top pattern. |

## Round Bottom Pattern

### Pattern Characteristics
- Also called "saucers"; form at market bottoms
- Form after prolonged down-trend with slow, gradual markets
- Relatively reliable patterns, easy to spot (in curve form) at bottom
- Weak volume markets during formation
- Trading similar to "rectangle" pattern; better visible in higher time-frames
- Trend line drawn connecting "swing highs" of round bottom to initiate trades at breakout levels
- Round bottoms with long base may provide more profitable trades

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade Entry** | Draw trend line connecting previous "swing highs" prior to Round bottom. Price trading above trend line signals breakout and long trade. Enter long above high of breakout bar from trend line. |
| **Target**      | First target: Depth of Round bottom pattern from breakout level. Second target: Previous "major swing highs".                                                                                   |
| **Stop Loss**   | Pattern failure occurs if market closes below trend line for series of bars. Place stop below trend line to protect trade.                                                                      |

## V-Top Pattern

### Pattern Characteristics
- V-Top and V-Bottom are extreme patterns, easy to spot
- V-top (Inverted V): ascending market action and angle of ascent very sharp with increased volume
- Once top is made, sharp price reversal occurs; descending angle usually mirrors ascending angle
- Clear signs at top (Dark Cloud or Island Top pattern) before reversal
- Most of the time, prices retrace all the way down to start of rally for true inverted V formation
- Easy to spot but challenging to trade due to steep ascent and fear factor
- Trend line drawn connecting swing lows in V-Top pattern; breakdown below offers short opportunity

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade Entry** | Connect "swing lows" in ascent of V-top pattern. Close below trend line signals breakdown. Enter short below low of breakdown bar.       |
| **Target**      | After price reversal, V-top patterns tend to travel in similar angle as ascent angle. Target set at "swing low" of pattern before rally. |
| **Stop Loss**   | Place stop above close of trend line. Longer-term stop above high of V-top Pattern.                                                      |

## V-Bottom Pattern

### Pattern Characteristics
- Resembles "V" shape
- Formed after steep decline followed by rally to reach top of pattern
- Form quite often; not always easy to detect until part of formation is complete
- Usually have single day of spike run-down or exhaustion sell-offs before rally begins
- Usually produce high volume on "spike" or "exhaustion" day, followed by even higher volume on following days
- Daily prices usually do not attempt to close near "low" of thrust days to signal potential reversal
- Trend line drawn connecting previous "swing highs" in V-bottom pattern
- Confirmation signaled when price closes above trend line

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                         |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Trade Entry** | Wait for confirmation above trend line. Enter long above high of breakout bar.                               |
| **Stop Loss**   | V-bottom failure occurs when prices close below low of V-bottom pattern.                                     |
| **Target**      | V-bottom patterns usually return to prices at top before another descent. Set targets near "top" of pattern. |

## Dragon Pattern

### Pattern Characteristics
- Similar to W-Pattern or Double Bottom pattern with different trading rules and targets
- "Inverse Dragon" patterns similar to M-pattern
- Usually form at market bottoms
- Work in all time-frames and all market instruments
- Present excellent trading opportunities with low risk to reward ratios

### Formation Structure
1. Pattern starts with "Head" formation
2. Price declines from head level form two legs of the "Dragon"
3. Two legs usually form within 5%-10% of price difference
4. Second leg has strong indication of reversal: key reversal bar or divergence in oscillator indicators
5. Price rise in second leg usually followed by spike in volume
6. Trend line drawn connecting head of Dragon to hump
7. When price closes above trend line and confirmed by price action or oscillator divergence, signals reversal
8. Second confirmation: price closes above hump, 38%-50% of range from head to low of first leg

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                                |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade Entry** | Aggressive: enter long when price closes above trend line. Better entry: when price closes above hump level. Enter long few ticks above hump level. |
| **Target**      | First target: 127% of second leg range. Second target: near "head level."                                                                           |
| **Stop Loss**   | Place stop below "lowest low" of two legs.                                                                                                          |

## Gaps

### Overview
- Formed when price imbalance occurs in buy and sell orders
- Usually driven by news, economic reports, earnings reports, or upgrades/downgrades
- Daily gaps defined in stocks: day's opening price (9:30 am EST) higher/lower than prior day's close (4 pm EST)
- Most stocks and index futures gap about 20%-25% of time during year
- Gap trading is profitable but relatively difficult; many traders avoid due to manipulation by professional/institutional traders
- Experienced traders employ successful strategies by fading gaps; watch pivots, support/resistance within gaps
- Gaps form during intra-day trading (time-based charts) and may offer potential clues to price-action
- Higher time frame gaps (30-min, 60-min) offer excellent trading opportunities

### Gap Types (Primary Grouping)

| Gap Type             | Characteristics                                                                                                                                                        | Trading Approach                                                                                                                        |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Common Gap**       | Form in quiet, trendless markets with small volume increase; occur most often                                                                                          | Avoid by most traders; experienced traders may fade with quick entry/exit                                                               |
| **Breakaway Gap**    | Most reliable; easy to visually detect; occur when prices rise out of congestion zones/long base areas; heavy volume on breakout; do not close quickly; initiate trend | Enter at beginning of trend; stop below low of bar prior to gap; target previous major swing high; close after 3-4 days upside movement |
| **Continuation Gap** | Prices make new highs/lows without filling gap for extended periods; signal continuation of existing trend; form in middle of trend; heavy volume                      | Trade in direction of established trend; relatively easy as trend direction clearly established                                         |
| **Exhaustion Gap**   | Occur near end of trend; not followed by new highs/lows after gap bar; great reversal opportunities; markets fall/rise violently after formation                       | Enter short below low of gap bar (for exhaustion top); stop above swing high; target near swing lows prior to exhaustion gap rally      |

### Basic Gap Trading Rules

1. Trading Gaps is an art and specialty by itself
2. Gap trading methods offer reasonable success but present more risk than other methods; however, breakaway, exhaustion, and island reversals offer excellent rewards
3. Stocks for gap trading must have consistent trading range:
   - $20 stock: at least $0.50 average trading range (ATR)
   - $50 stock: at least $1 average trading range
   - $100 stock: at least $2 average trading range
4. For upside breakaway gap consideration: stock must be closing lower at least 2-3 days prior to gap formation
5. Gaps below markets act as support; above markets act as resistance
6. In most cases, index futures' opening gaps tend to fill during same trading day
7. Common gap trading should be avoided by most traders
8. During Gap trading, protect trades using trail stops
9. Valid breakaway gap almost never gets filled; "all Gaps get filled" is only partially true
10. If daily stock gap is 2.5x the 10-day ATR, ignore trade until at least following day

## Dead Cat Bounce Pattern

### Pattern Characteristics
- Event-driven pattern occurring due to sudden, major event or news:
  - Corporate earnings reports, mergers, takeovers, partnerships
  - Resignations/appointments, scandals
  - FDA approvals, SEC inquiries
- From regular trading price pattern, price usually drops or rises at least 15% or more to indicate seriousness and reaction to event
- As condition emerges, traders enter trade in opposite direction of event and trade a rise/drop about half of event price change at leisurely rate = "Dead Cat Bounce"
- Once news settles, price reverses the bounce and trades in original event-driven direction
- Has bull and bearish setups
- High failure rate as some gaps are never retraced or filled for very long time

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade Entry** | Easier to trade once Dead Cat Bounce occurs rather than chasing event-driven price-action. Rise/drop must be at least 15% of price. Most bounces are 50%-62% of drop against event-day direction. For event-driven price-fall: enter short at 50%-62% gap level; short below previous bar's low. For event-driven price-rise: enter long at 50%-62% gap level; enter long above previous bar's high. |
| **Stop Loss**   | Place stop at event-day's gap levels. For short trade: stop at high of event-day's gap down bar. For long trade: stop below low of event-day's gap up bar.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| **Target**      | Target placed about 100% of gap range from trade entry.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |

## Island Reversal Pattern

### Pattern Characteristics
- Part of price gap structures
- Form when prices are isolated by gaps on both sides of price rally or decline
- In extended rally: price rallies above prior session's close forming upside gap; after few sessions, downside gap forms closing below prior session's close
- This isolation of price-range forms Island Reversal setup, usually signaling much larger technical declines in bullish trends, or rallies in bearish trends
- Most Island reversals are usually news-driven events
- Volume should be at higher levels on both sides of gaps
- Very rare to see Island Reversal pattern in middle of rally; they reverse prior trends after second gap

### Trading Rules

| Element         | Rule                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Trade Entry** | After reversal: Enter short (for uptrend) below low of second down gap. Or, enter long (in downtrend) above high of second gap up.                    |
| **Stop Loss**   | Place stop if market closes above high of Island Reversal pattern for shorts, or if it closes below low of Island Reversal pattern for longs.         |
| **Target**      | Island Reversals usually very profitable as they signal weakness in current trend. Targets placed near key event-driven support or resistance levels. |

## Definitions Glossary

| Term                | Definition                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Breakdown**       | Current price surpassing a series of support prices and trading below it                                                                                                   |
| **Breakout**        | Current Price overcoming a series of resistance prices and trading above it                                                                                                |
| **Congestion**      | Market in short-term and choppy price-range                                                                                                                                |
| **Divergence**      | When two or more averages or indices fail to show confirming trends                                                                                                        |
| **Drawdown**        | The reduction in account equity as a result of a trade or series of trades                                                                                                 |
| **Entry**           | A new trade (Could be "Buy" or "Sell")                                                                                                                                     |
| **Exit**            | Closing the trade (Could be "Sell" or "Buy to Cover")                                                                                                                      |
| **Extension**       | Price levels surpassing a previous Swing in the same direction as prior swing                                                                                              |
| **Fib.**            | Abbreviation for Fibonacci                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Fibonacci Ratio** | The ratio between any two successive numbers in the Fibonacci sequence, known as phi (φ)                                                                                   |
| **Fractals**        | Depiction of mathematical models that may be applied to identify data patterns                                                                                             |
| **Gap**             | Current Price Opening away (Up or Down) from the previous bar's Close                                                                                                      |
| **Impulse Wave**    | A wave or cycle of waves that carries the current trend further in the same direction                                                                                      |
| **Long**            | A Buy entry                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Minute Chart**    | The time period for the chart is [n] minutes                                                                                                                               |
| **Neckline**        | A trendline drawn along the support or resistance points of various reversal and consolidation patterns (i.e., head and shoulder, double and triple top/bottom formations) |
| **Opening Range**   | The range of prices that occur during the first five minutes to first hour of trading, depending on the preference of the individual analyst                               |
| **Overbought**      | Market prices that have risen too steeply and too fast                                                                                                                     |
| **Oversold**        | Market prices that have declined too steeply and too fast                                                                                                                  |
| **Pivot**           | A key price level where prices change or reverse its prior direction                                                                                                       |
| **Range**           | The distance between two price points (usually High and Low)                                                                                                               |
| **Resistance**      | Prices where selling pressure accumulates                                                                                                                                  |
| **Retracement**     | Price reversing from an upside advance or a downside decline (within a swing)                                                                                              |
| **Short**           | A Sell entry                                                                                                                                                               |
| **Spike**           | A sharp rise in price in a single day or two; may be as great as 15-30%, indicating the time for an immediate sale                                                         |
| **Stop Loss**       | The risk management technique in which the trade is liquidated to halt any further decline in value                                                                        |
| **Support**         | Prices where buying pressure accumulates                                                                                                                                   |
| **Swing**           | The distance between two key pivot points (continuous)                                                                                                                     |
| **Target**          | A profit price level from a trade entry                                                                                                                                    |
| **Tick**            | The minimum price movement in a stock or contract                                                                                                                          |
| **Trendline**       | A line drawn that connects either a series of highs or lows in a trend                                                                                                     |
