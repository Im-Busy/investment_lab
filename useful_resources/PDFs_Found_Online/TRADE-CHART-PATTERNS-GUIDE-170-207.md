## ZigZag Patterns

### Overview
Market prices move in a non-linear format and form "peaks" and "troughs" to signal the end-of-trends and the begin-of-trends. Connecting these "peaks" and "troughs" generate a pattern called "ZigZag," which are used in:

- **Elliott Waves**: To detect various waves, using Fibonacci retracement to detect "price" clustering sequences
- **Price Pattern Detection**: Such as "M" and "W" patterns

### Construction Method
The construction of "ZigZag" patterns includes:
1. Finding the key peaks and troughs with pivots
2. Determining the "ZigZag" leg lengths, elapsed time, and bar count

### Key Characteristics
> ⚠️ "ZigZag" patterns are eye pleasing and tempting but they provide **no predictive analysis by themselves**. They can be coupled with pattern recognition techniques or other theories like overlaying on Renko charting to assist with trading.

**Benefits:**
- Filter out noise
- Compare each leg's size with previous swings to assess trend strength
- Use variables to validate patterns:
  - Minimum price movement (points or percentage)
  - Average True Range (ATR) for volatility measurement
  - "Pivot" strengths

### Applications
- Price cluster generation to determine potential support and resistance areas
- Automatically drawn on charts to plot Fibonacci ratios for prior swings
- Referenced theories: J.D. Hamon and Michael Gur's "ZigZag" and "Symmetric wave" theory trading

> 📌 **Note**: Because "ZigZag" patterns are used only for trend indicators, there are no specific trading rules provided.

## 9.2 Elliott Wave

### 🖼️ Illustration Description: Elliott Wave Pattern Diagram
*[Visual: Schematic diagram showing 5-wave impulse pattern followed by 3-wave correction]*

**Historical Background:**
In the late 1920's, Ralph Elliott discovered that markets have a rhythm and travel in a series of wave patterns. Elliott suggested markets move in:
- **5 strength waves (impulse)**
- **3 correction waves**

### Wave Descriptions

| Wave | Name | Characteristics |
|------|------|----------------|
| **Wave 1 (W1)** | Initiation | Smallest of Elliott waves; start of the "fractal wave" pattern |
| **Wave 2 (W2)** | Retracement | Should not trade below lows of Wave 1 |
| **Wave 3 (W3)** | Momentum | Longest and strongest of entire wave count; must trade above high of Wave 1 |
| **Wave 4 (W4)** | Profit-taking | "Profit wave" – traders who entered at W1 take profits; does not trade below W2's high |
| **Wave 5 (W5)** | Exhaustion | "Greed wave" or "overpriced wave"; exhaustion price movements occur before serious correction |

**Post-5-Wave Pattern:**
After 5 wave patterns, markets form an **ABC pattern** in a three corrective waves fashion before another series begins.

> ⚠️ The Elliott Wave theory is complex and very subjective for each trader. This pattern occurs in all markets and in all time-frames.

**Trading Application:**
- Basic theory: Markets move in waves in the direction of primary trends
- Cycle structure: 5 Action waves + 3 Reaction waves
- Wave ranges can be determined by Fibonacci numbers for time/price ranges
- Some traders plot "wave channels" to determine entry and target areas

> ⚠️ Elliott Wave theory is very subjective and requires careful understanding of the entire wave theory thesis to be successful.

## Crown Pattern

### Pattern Overview
"Crown" patterns are a variation of skewed "Head and Shoulders" patterns that incorporate Fibonacci ratios in their formation.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Form at the end of market rallies or declines, suggesting a major trend change
- Resemble a series of "spikes" formed with Fibonacci ratios compared to prior swings
- Form in all markets and all time-frames
- Moderately reliable but need confirmation to trade
- Occasionally form multiple spikes like "Zigzag" in a short span before the pattern fully emerges from the channel

### Trading Rules

#### Bearish Crown Pattern
| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Trade Entry** | Setup made below the low of the recent "swing low" |
| **Stop Loss** | Place order 2 ticks above the "swing high" of the pattern |
| **Target** | Profitable trades can be trailed with a 2-bar high for short trades; set profit targets at major "swing lows" |

#### Bullish Crown Pattern
| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Trade Entry** | Setup made above the high of the recent "swing high" |
| **Stop Loss** | Place order below the low of recent "swing low" |
| **Target** | Trail with a 2-bar low for long trades; set targets at major "swing highs" |

### 10.1 Cup and Handle Pattern

#### Pattern Overview
"Cup and Handle" is a **continuation pattern** that usually forms in bullish markets. Most "Cup and Handle" patterns are very reliable and offer great trading opportunities.

**Formation Structure:**
- **Cup**: Developed during price rallies from a round bottom
- **Handle**: Forms due to price correction before a clear breakout to the upside

**Key Measurements:**
- Handle (right side) usually corrects about **25% to 38%** (of Cup depth) from the high pivot point to the bottom of the Cup
- After correction (handle), pattern resumes prior trend and trades in breakout fashion above the 'top' of the 'Cup'

#### Trading Rules

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Trade Entry** | Best opportunity when price clears top of "Cup"; enter "long" when price closes above high of the cup |
| **Target** | Most patterns result in 62% to 100% of Cup depth from breakout levels |
| **Stop Loss** | Place "stop" order below the low of the handle to protect the trade |

> 💡 Trading "Cup and Handle" is a two-fold process: (1) Handle can be traded as corrective pattern; (2) Continuation pattern after breakout from top of Cup

## Head and Shoulders Pattern

### Pattern Overview
"Head and Shoulders" patterns are **reversal formations** that usually form at market tops.

**Reliability Note:**
- Very reliable, but failures do occur
- When patterns fail, they reverse and trade in an explosive manner

### Volume Analysis
Most "Head and Shoulders" patterns can be detected using volume patterns:
- **Left Shoulder + Head Formation**: Heavy volume
- **Right Shoulder Formation**: Volume dissipates
- **Breakdown**: Volume increases

### Neckline Construction
- Trend line or neckline drawn connecting "Head and Shoulders" pattern bottoms
- Determines potential trade opportunities and targets
- Neckline can be formed at an angle (slanted)

#### Trading Rules (Bearish H&S)

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Trade Entry** | Connect bottoms in trend line/neckline; when price closes below neckline, short one tick below breakdown bar's low |
| **Target** | Compute vertical distance between "apex" of Head and neckline; set target below this distance from neckline |
| **Stop Loss** | If price closes above neckline after entry, pattern failure signaled; place stop above neckline |

## Parabolic Arc Pattern

### Pattern Overview
"Parabolic arc" is a **reversal pattern** with very predictable outcomes.

**Market Behavior:**
- After parabolic base, prices move up vertically
- Acceleration eventually stops and reverses
- Prices show lower-lows and may attempt to regain the top again
- Most patterns have significant correction of **62% from the top**

**Trading Challenge:**
Although predictable, relatively difficult to trade since market sentiment is bullish and may be tough to identify reversal points.

#### Trading Rules

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Trade Entry** | Enter "short" at second failed attempt to test peak OR at trend line breakdown connecting major swing lows |
| **Target** | 62% retracement from peak |
| **Stop Loss** | Place few ticks above high point of Parabolic Arc |

## 10.4 Three Hills and A Mountain Pattern

### Pattern Overview
The "Three Hills and A Mountain" is **rare but very reliable**.

**Pattern Characteristics:**
- Similar to "3-Drives (Bearish)" pattern, but hills are not of equal magnitude
- Consists of another reversal trade in original trend direction
- Close relation to Elliott Wave theory
- Supports Fibonacci retracement and extension concepts for key support/resistance areas

**Structure:**
- Three distinct "hills" with Fibonacci-proportionate retracements
- Each hill retraces to minimum **0.5 to 0.618** (Fibonacci ratio) of its height before expanding into next hill
- After completion of "Three Hills," trend line drawn connecting bottoms
- Close below trend line gives short-trading opportunity
- After 62% retracement of prior range, "Mountain" rallies about **100% to 127%** of entire hills range (AB)

#### Trading Rules

| Trade | Entry | Target | Stop |
|-------|-------|--------|------|
| **First Trade (Short)** | Close below trend line | 62% of prior swing length "AB" | Above "B" level |
| **Second Trade (Long)** | After 62% retracement to downside at "C" | 100% to 127% of "AB" to upside from "C" | Below "C" level |

## 10.5 Three Valleys and A River Pattern

### Pattern Overview
The "Three Valleys and A River" pattern is:
- Similar to "Three Hills and A Mountain" but in reverse sense
- Similar to "3-Drives (bullish)" pattern
- **Very rare but very reliable**

**Structure:**
- Three distinct valleys as prices make three thrusts upward, succeeding on third attempt
- Usually occurs near market bottoms
- Supports Fibonacci ratios among the three drives
- Each valley retraces to minimum **0.5 to 0.618** Fibonacci ratio of its height before expanding into next valley
- After three valleys completion: trend line drawn connecting tops
- Prices closing above trend line signal "long" trade
- After 62% of previous range completion: significant reversal to downside ("River")

#### Trading Rules

| Trade | Entry | Target | Stop |
|-------|-------|--------|------|
| **First Trade (Long)** | Close above trend line; enter one-tick above high of breakout bar | 62% to 78% of AB range | Close below trend line |
| **Second Trade (Short)** | After 62% of AB range completion at point "C" | 100% of AB range retracement from point "C" | Close above level "C" |

## Spike and Ledge Pattern

### Pattern Overview
The "Spike and Ledge" pattern was first defined by **Larry Connors and Linda Raschke** in the book *Street Smarts*.

**Pattern Structure:**
1. **Spike**: Markets make a "climax high" or "climax low" (New High/New Low)
2. **Ledge**: Followed by a resting point with matching highs/lows
3. **Reversal**: Then reversal of prior trend

### Ledge Pattern Definition
- Series of bars with matching lows and highs within certain number of bars (typically 20)
- Considered resting point before markets pick clear direction (mostly reversal of current major trend)
- Effective in all time-frames, but **more effective during intra-day trading**

#### Trading Rules

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| **Trade Entry** | Set entry at "breakout" or "breakdown" from Ledge formation; wait for clear signal and enter at low/high of this bar |
| **Stop Loss** | Place stop on opposite side of Ledge: below low for long trades; above high for short trades |
| **Target** | Prior "major swing high" (long) or "major swing low" (short) from breakdown/breakout levels; if gap prior to Spike, target gap levels; otherwise target prior swing before Spike |
