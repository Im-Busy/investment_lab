Page 1: A Multimodal Event-driven LSTM Model for
Page 1: Stock Prediction Using Online News
Page 1: Qing Li, Member, IEEE, Jinghua Tan, Member, IEEE, Jun Wang, Hsinchun Chen, Fellow, IEEE
Page 1: AbstractΓÇöIn finance, it is believed that market information, namely, fundamentals and news information, affects stock movements.
Page 1: Such media-aware stock movements essentially comprise a multimodal problem. Two unique challenges arise in processing these
Page 1: multimodal data. First, information from one data mode will interact with information from other data modes. A common strategy is to
Page 1: concatenate various data modes into one compound vector; however, this strategy ignores the interactions among different modes.
Page 1: The second challenge is the heterogeneity of the data in terms of sampling time. Specifically, fundamental data consist of continuous
Page 1: values sampled at fixed time intervals, whereas news information emerges randomly. This heterogeneity can cause valuable information
Page 1: to be partially missing or can distort the feature spaces. In addition, the study of media-aware stock movements in previous work has
Page 1: focused on the one-to-one problem, in which it is assumed that news affects only the performance of the stocks mentioned in the reports.
Page 1: However, news articles also impact related stocks and cause stock co-movements. In this article, we propose a tensor-based eventdriven LSTM model to address these challenges. Experiments performed on the China securities market demonstrate the superiority of
Page 1: the proposed approach over state-of-the-art algorithms, including AZFinText, eMAQT, and TeSIA.
Page 1: Index TermsΓÇöStock Prediction, Tensor, Multimodality, Deep Learning, LSTM.

Page 2: effects on related companies. However, the fluctuation of

Page 2: instance, a news report on the alternative energy supply on

Page 2: PetroChina (601857) stock, resulting in a decrease of 1.35%.
Page 2: By contrast, due to its savings on transportation costs, Air
Page 2: China (601111) saw its stock increase by 15.35%. Incorporating such correlations among relevant companies to quantify

Page 2: In this section, we review the relevant literature from

Page 2: volatility, stock comovements and the approaches for quantifying such media-aware movements.

Page 2: Fundamental information: A number of studies in
Page 2: traditional finance have examined the effects of fundamental information. Haugen and Baker showed
Page 2: that cash flows can provide additional information
Page 2: content for better understanding stock markets [21].
Page 2: Fama and French found that a stockΓÇÖs performance is
Page 2: determined mainly by three risk factors: the overall
Page 2: market, the firm size, and the book-to-market equity
Page 2: ratio (BE/ME) [22]. Jegadeesh and Titman observed
Page 2: that stocks with higher returns in the previous 12
Page 2: months tended to have higher future returns [23].
Page 2: Media information: The pilot research on mediaaware stock movements can be traced back to work
Page 2: on the influence of financial reports on stocks [24].
Page 2: Later, researchers observed the influence of online
Page 2: media on stock fluctuations [15, 20]. In particular,
Page 2: investorsΓÇÖ decisions can be influenced by the opinions of others as expressed via online media, which
Page 2: may result in herd behavior in investment. For example, Schumaker and Chen experimented with several
Page 2: textual news representation approaches to study the
Page 2: effects of breaking news on stock movements [10].
Page 2: Bollen, Mao and Zeng found that the collective mood
Page 2: states derived from 10 million tweets were correlated
Page 2: with the index of the Dow Jones Industrial Average
Page 2: (DJIA) using a self-organizing fuzzy neural network
Page 2: (SOFNN) model [14]. These works have prompted
Page 2: the birth of media-aware hedge funds, including
Page 2: Derwent Capital Markets, DCM Capital and Cayman
Page 2: Atlantic.
Page 2: Combination: Many studies have shown that both
Page 2: fundamental and media information can shape stock
Page 2: movements. However, difficulties arise in modeling
Page 2: these two types of information [25, 26]. One common
Page 2: strategy is to concatenate these information into a
Page 2: compound vector, thus treating each value as an independent variable. For example, Tetlock measured
Page 2: the positive (negative) sentiment polarity of an article
Page 2: and applied a linear regression model to capture
Page 2: stock returns [12]. Mittermayer and Knolmayer represented market information spaces with vectors and
Page 2: applied SVM and KNN models to study the impacts
Page 2: of news on stock markets [15]. However, such vectorbased methods dilute or even ignore the intrinsic
Page 2: associations among various information sources. Alternatively, researchers have found that tensor representations are able to capture the interconnections
Page 2: among various modes of market information, thus
Page 2: providing a better understanding of stock movements. Li et al. was the first to apply tensor theory
Page 2: to model the complicated market information space
Page 2: and show that such a representation is able to capture
Page 2: the joint effects of different information sources [16].

Page 3: Stock comovements
Page 3: 
Page 3: As discussed in regard to the above examples of PetroChina
Page 3: and Air China, it is of great interest to consider the influence
Page 3: of related companies on stock movements. The challenge
Page 3: here lies in how to identify related companies. Related to
Page 3: this challenge is the work on stock comovements.
Page 3: There are two mainstream methods of studying stock comovements: from the perspective of fundamentals and from
Page 3: the perspective of investor behavior. Traditional financial
Page 3: researchers have attributed stock comovments to the fundamental characteristics of the listed companies [27, 28, 29].
Page 3: For instance, Pindyck and Rotemberg discovered that company size and the degree of institutional ownership influence stock comovements [27]. Preis et al. reported that stock
Page 3: correlations are reflected by normalized DJIA index returns
Page 3: on various time scales [28]. Aghabozorgi and Teh attributed
Page 3: stock comovements to historical transaction prices [29]. In
Page 3: contrast, in modern behavioral finance, it is believed that
Page 3: irrational behaviors of investors cause the comovements
Page 3: of related stocks. For example, Rashes found a highly abnormal positive correlation between two companies with
Page 3: similar names but nothing else in common, caused by the
Page 3: irrational feelings of investors [30].
Page 3: With the advancement of the Web 2.0 era, the influence of
Page 3: online information on stocks has become salient [9]. The influence of online media involves two aspects: fundamentals
Page 3: and emotions. Web media enrich investorsΓÇÖ knowledge by
Page 3: conveying a more comprehensive view of a firmΓÇÖs financial
Page 3: standing. In addition, Web media provide a platform for
Page 3: expressing the options of experts and the public moods
Page 3: of investors, which inevitably affect investor behavior and
Page 3: 
Page 3: can even elicit herd behavior [9]. Essentially, Web media
Page 3: act as a sort of mirror reflecting the fundamentals of listed
Page 3: companies and affecting investor behavior to some degree.
Page 3: In this study, we build an innovative media-based enterprise
Page 3: network to identify related companies in terms of their
Page 3: media performance.

Page 3: Stock analysis models
Page 3: 
Page 3: Once fundamental data and news information have been
Page 3: obtained in a machine-friendly form, various types of analysis models can be applied to study media-aware stock movements. There are three mainstream classes of such models:
Page 3: statistical models (originating from statistics), regression
Page 3: models (originating from econometrics) and machine learning models (originating from computer science). Table 1
Page 3: summarizes the related work in terms of these classes of

Page 3: its stock performance [33]. However, both statistical models

Page 3: to capture their joint effects on stock performance. Thus,

Page 3: machine learning algorithms to capture such complex nonlinear relationships.

Page 4: M ODEL ARCHITECTURE
Page 4: 
Page 4: Stock markets are influenced by various information
Page 4: sources, including fundamental data and media information. A common strategy in previous studies has been
Page 4: to concatenate information from these heterogeneous data
Page 4: sources into a compound vector. However, these vectorbased models treat different information sources as independent features, ignoring the inherent links between them and
Page 4: thus failing to properly capture stock movements [16]. In
Page 4: addition, fundamental data are continuous values sampled
Page 4: at equal time intervals, whereas media information emerges
Page 4: randomly. This heterogeneity results in valuable information being partially missing. Therefore, we use tensors to
Page 4: represent market information to preserve the multifaceted
Page 4: and interrelated nature of the data. On this basis, a multimodal event-driven LSTM model is proposed to capture the
Page 4: nonlinear relations between market information and stock
Page 4: movements. Figure 2 shows an overview of the proposed
Page 4: approach.

Page 7: Multimodal event-driven LSTM model
Page 7: 
Page 7: A long short-term memory (LSTM) model is a variant of
Page 7: a recurrent neural network (RNN) that is able to handle
Page 7: long-term dependencies by means of a gate mechanism [46].
Page 7: Such a model is designed to handle time sequence data collected at equal intervals, such as daily transaction data [20].
Page 7: However, predicting media-driven stock movements is essentially a multimodal problem, in which each mode has
Page 7: unique characteristics. Specifically, the information space
Page 7: for stock markets consists of both fundamental information
Page 7: and media information. The fundamental data are daily
Page 7: transaction records sampled at equal intervals. In contrast,
Page 7: the data for the media mode consist of discrete values
Page 7: sampled at nonequal time intervals due to the random time
Page 7: distribution of news releases. This randomness can lead
Page 7: to failure of the long-term dependency mechanism of an
Page 7: LSTM model [47]. Specifically, if two similar news articles
Page 7: are released with a sufficiently large time gap, the LSTM
Page 7: model may forget the knowledge learned from the first news
Page 7: article before processing the information from the second. To
Page 7: solve this problem, we propose a novel event-driven LSTM
Page 7: model by extending an LSTM model to include an eventdriven memory mechanism. In addition, we adopt a tensor
Page 7: representation to capture the interactions among different
Page 7: modes of the multimodal data.

Page 11: 4.5.1
Page 11: 
Page 11: Effectiveness of the tensor representation
Page 11: 
Page 11: As mentioned above, both fundamental and media information can shape stock movements. Predicting media-aware
Page 11: stock movements is essentially a multimodal problem. Difficulties arise in attempting to model the two relevant types
Page 11: of information without ignoring the interactions among
Page 11: different data types. One common strategy is to concatenate
Page 11: multiple types of information into one compound vector,
Page 11: thus inevitably diluting or even ignoring the intrinsic associations between the two information sources. In this study,
Page 11: we modeled market information in the form of tensors
Page 11: to retain the interactions between different modes when
Page 11: addressing the multimodal learning problem. To investigate the effectiveness of this tensor representation, we first

Page 11: To capture media-aware stock movements, accounting for
Page 11: the heterogeneity of the sampling times between the two
Page 11: information modes is a critical issue for this multimodal
Page 11: learning problem. Specifically, the fundamental data consist

Page 13: C ONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORK
Page 13: 
Page 13: Stock markets are strongly affected by various types of
Page 13: highly interrelated information. In both traditional finance
Page 13: 
Page 13: and behavioral finance, it is believed that market information, especially fundamental information and news report
Page 13: information, shapes stock movements. Predicting future
Page 13: stock trends based on market information is essentially
Page 13: a multimodal data problem. Multimodal data consist of
Page 13: several modes, each corresponding to a group of similar
Page 13: data sharing the same attributes. In this study, market
Page 13: information data are considered to consist of two modes:
Page 13: fundamentals and news. Two unique challenges arise in
Page 13: processing these multimodal data. The first challenge is that
Page 13: the information from one data mode interacts with information from other data modes, violating the assumption of feature independence that is adopted in traditional supervised
Page 13: learning. A common strategy in previous studies has been to
Page 13: concatenate the information from various data modes into a
Page 13: compound vector, thereby ignoring the interactions among
Page 13: different modes. By contrast, in this study, we proposed a
Page 13: tensor representation approach for modeling multimodal
Page 13: market information. This method is able to preserve the
Page 13: interrelations between fundamental and news information
Page 13: and to capture their joint effects. The second challenge is
Page 13: the sampling heterogeneity of the different data modes.
Page 13: For market information, fundamental data are continuous
Page 13: values sampled at fixed time intervals, whereas news information emerges randomly. This heterogeneity can result in a
Page 13: partial loss of valuable information and can even distort the
Page 13: feature space. In this study, we proposed an event-driven
Page 13: memory mechanism to address the sampling heterogeneity among different data sources for multimodal learning.
Page 13: Experiments performed on an entire year of data from
Page 13: the China securities market demonstrate the superiority
Page 13: of the proposed approach over state-of-the-art algorithms,
Page 13: including AZFinText, eMAQT, and TeSIA, and our method
Page 13: achieved a return of 136.42% in an investment simulation.
Page 13: In this study, we focused on media-aware stock movements. However, the proposed tensor-based event-driven
Page 13: LSTM framework can be generalizable to many other multimodal learning problems in which the information space
Page 13: consists of several interacting data modes with sampling
Page 13: heterogeneity. For instance, in health care monitoring, both
Page 13: daily monitoring indicators and random sickness records
Page 13: are applied to detect health abnormalities [47]. Another
Page 13: good example is the prediction of crop growth in agriculture
Page 13: based on daily growth indicators and uncertain conditions,
Page 13: including rainfall, wind and disasters [48]. However, the
Page 13: effectiveness of the proposed method in related fields has
Page 13: yet to be explored.
