# TECHNICAL ANALYSIS

## WHAT IS TECHNICAL ANALYSIS?
- Studying stock price graphs and a few momentum oscillators.
- Based entirely on prices
- Do not include Balance Sheets, P&L Accounts (fundamental analysis)
- The assumption being that the markets are efficient and all possible price sensitive information is built into the price graph of a security/ index.
- Exclusive use of historical data.

## ASSUMPTIONS OF TECHNICAL ANALYSIS
1. **Market discounts everything**
   - Only considers price movements, ignores fundamental factors.
   - Assumes stock price reflects everything.
   - All fundamentals are priced into the stock.
2. **Prices moves in trends**
   - Price movements are assumed to follow particular trend.
   - Most technical strategies are based on this assumption.
3. **History trends to repeat itself**
   - Market participants provide consistent reaction to similar market stimuli over time.

## DOW THEORY
The Dow theory on stock price movement is a form of technical analysis.

The theory was derived from 255 Wall Street Journal editorials written by Charles H. Dow, journalist, founder and first editor of the Wall Street Journal and co-founder of Dow Jones and Company.

**Hypothesis:** Dow Theory is based on the hypothesis that the stock market does not perform on a random basis. Rather, it is guided by some specific trends.

### Three types of specific trends have been named in Dow Theory:
- **PRIMARY TREND:** Primary movement or major trend may last from less than a year to several years. It can be bullish or bearish.
- **SECONDARY TREND:** Intermediate corrections against the primary trend, typically lasting from three weeks to three months. *(Note: Original PDF text contains a duplication error here; this is the corrected definition.)*
- **MINOR TREND:** Day to day trend or movements in prices over few days. It is of very short duration.

### BASIC ASSUMPTIONS
- Market price determined by demand and supply forces.
- Prices move in trend for long periods.
- Reversal or shift in price trends may occur.
- Charts and graphs can predict change in demand and supply forces.
- Price patterns tends to repeat themselves.

## CHARTING: THE BASIC TOOL
- Motive of identifying price trends based on historical data.
- Trend used to forecast future behaviour.
- Used for either a particular security or market in general.
- Both price and volume data are studied simultaneously for both (security as well as the market).

## ELLIOT WAVE THEORY
- Developed by Ralph Nelson Elliot.
- Theory states that the long term major patterns may consist of five successive steps or five waves.
- **Types of market:**
  - Bull market
  - Bear market

### ELLIOT WAVE THEORY IN BULL MARKET
*[Illustration Description: A line chart depicting the classic 5-3 Elliot Wave pattern in a bull market. The impulse phase shows five waves: Wave 1 (upward move), Wave 2 (corrective downward), Wave 3 (strongest upward move, typically longest), Wave 4 (minor corrective downward), Wave 5 (final upward move). This is followed by a corrective ABC phase: Wave A (downward), Wave B (upward retracement), Wave C (final downward move). Each wave is labeled numerically or alphabetically, with trendlines connecting wave peaks and troughs to show the pattern structure.]*

### ELLIOT WAVE THEORY IN BEAR MARKET
*[Illustration Description: A line chart showing the inverse Elliot Wave pattern for a bear market. The impulse phase consists of five downward waves: Wave 1 (downward), Wave 2 (corrective upward), Wave 3 (strongest downward move), Wave 4 (minor upward correction), Wave 5 (final downward move). This is followed by a three-wave corrective upward pattern: Wave A (upward), Wave B (downward retracement), Wave C (final upward move). Labels and trendlines mirror the bull market structure but inverted.]*

### REAL LIFE EXAMPLE
*[Illustration Description: An actual stock price chart with Elliot Wave labels overlaid. The chart shows historical price data with annotated wave counts (1, 2, 3, 4, 5, A, B, C) marking the identified impulse and corrective phases. Trendlines connect key pivot points to demonstrate how the theoretical pattern manifests in real market data.]*

## GREATER FOOL THEORY
The belief that we can always buy investments at any given price, setting aside valuations, and eventually turning them into a profit because there will always be a "Greater Fool" willing to pay the higher price.

## PRICE CHARTS
- Opening price
- High price
- Low price
- Closing price

### BAR CHARTS
- This is a popular technique of showing the price variation and volume on a particular day.
- The chart is made up of a series of vertical lines that represent each data point.
- This vertical line represents the high and low for the trading period, along with the closing price.
- The close and open are represented on the vertical line by a horizontal dash.
*[Illustration Description: A series of vertical bars, each representing one trading period. Each bar has a vertical line spanning from the low to high price. A small horizontal tick on the left side of the vertical line marks the opening price; a small horizontal tick on the right side marks the closing price. Bars are arranged chronologically from left to right.]*

### LINE CHART
- Line chart represents any variable over a set period of time.
- The line is formed by connecting value of represented variable over the time frame.
- Line charts do not provide visual information of the trading range for the individual points such as the high, low and opening prices and closing price.
- They depict any variable like volume of a security, index number, price etc.

#### Example for Line chart
*[Illustration Description: A simple two-dimensional graph with time (dates) on the horizontal x-axis and price values on the vertical y-axis. A single continuous line connects data points representing closing prices (or another single variable) at regular intervals. The line trends upward, downward, or sideways depending on price movement. No individual candlesticks or bars are shown—only the connected line.]*

### POINT AND FIGURE CHART
- The point and figure chart is not well known or used by the average investor but it has had a long history of use dating back to the first technical traders.
- This type of chart reflects price movements and is not as concerned about time and volume in the formulation of the points.
- In order to prepare this type of graph, the analyst has to decide as to what is a significant price change.
- It uses a chart with "X"s and "O"s for predicting financial asset prices. The "X"s are used to indicate rising prices and "O"s to indicate falling prices.

#### Example for Point and Figure chart
*[Illustration Description: A grid-based chart composed of columns of "X" and "O" symbols. Columns of X's represent rising prices and are stacked vertically upward; columns of O's represent falling prices and are stacked vertically downward. Columns alternate between X and O—only one symbol type appears per column. Price scale is shown on the vertical axis; time is not represented on the horizontal axis. A new column begins only when price reverses by a predetermined amount (the "box size").]*

### CANDLESTICK CHART
- Similar to the bar chart, the candlestick also has a thin vertical line showing the period's trading range.
- Candlesticks rely heavily on the use of colors to explain what has happened during the trading period. There are two color constructs for days up and one for days that the price falls.
- When the price of the stock is up and closes above the opening trade, the candlestick will usually be white or clear. If the stock has traded down for the period, then the candlestick will usually be red or black.
- If the stock's price has closed above the previous day's close but below the day's open, the candlestick will be black or filled with the color that is used to indicate an up day.

## SUPPORT AND RESISTANCE LEVELS
- **Support** is the price level at which demand is thought to be strong enough to prevent the price from declining further.
  - The logic dictates that as the price declines towards support and gets cheaper, buyers become more inclined to buy and sellers become less inclined to sell.
- **Resistance** is the price level at which selling is thought to be strong enough to prevent the price from rising further.
  - The logic dictates that as the price advances towards resistance, sellers become more inclined to sell and buyers become less inclined to buy.
*[Illustration Description: A price chart with two horizontal lines drawn across it. The lower line is labeled "Support" and shows price bouncing upward multiple times when it reaches this level. The upper line is labeled "Resistance" and shows price reversing downward multiple times upon reaching this level. Arrows may indicate buying pressure at support and selling pressure at resistance.]*

## REVERSAL PATTERNS AND CONTINUATION PATTERNS
- The reversal patterns are those which indicate a reversal of existing trend.
- It can further be classified as bullish patterns or bearish patterns.
- The continuation patterns suggest that there is only a pause in the market and the old trend will continue again after the pause.

### HEAD AND SHOULDER
- The head-and-shoulders top signals to chart users that a security's price is likely to make a downward move, especially after it breaks below the neckline of the pattern.
- Due to this pattern forming mostly at the peaks of upward trends, it is considered to be a trend-reversal pattern, as the security heads down after the pattern's completion.
*[Illustration Description: A price chart showing three peaks. The left shoulder is a moderate peak followed by a decline. The head is a higher peak followed by a decline to approximately the same trough level as after the left shoulder. The right shoulder is a lower peak (similar height to left shoulder) followed by a decline. A diagonal or horizontal "neckline" connects the two troughs between the peaks. An arrow shows price breaking downward through the neckline, signaling the reversal.]*

### INVERTED HEAD AND SHOULDER
- The inverted head-and-shoulders pattern is the exact opposite of the head-and-shoulders top, as it signals that the security is set to make an upward move.
- Often coming at the end of a downtrend, the inverse head and shoulders is considered to be a reversal pattern, as the security typically heads higher after the completion of the pattern.
*[Illustration Description: A price chart showing three troughs. The left shoulder is a moderate trough followed by a rise. The head is a deeper trough followed by a rise to approximately the same peak level as after the left shoulder. The right shoulder is a shallower trough (similar depth to left shoulder) followed by a rise. A "neckline" connects the two peaks between the troughs. An arrow shows price breaking upward through the neckline, signaling the bullish reversal.]*

### DOUBLE TOPS AND BOTTOMS
- These two reversal patterns illustrate a security's attempt to continue an existing trend.
- Upon several attempts to move higher, the trend is reversed and a new trend begins. These chart patterns formed will often resemble what looks like a "W" (for a double bottom) or an "M" (double top).

#### DOUBLE TOP
- The double-top pattern is found at the peaks of an upward trend and is a clear signal that the preceding upward trend is weakening and that buyers are losing interest.
- Upon completion of this pattern, the trend is considered to be reversed and the security is expected to move lower.
*[Illustration Description: A price chart forming an "M" shape. Price rises to a peak (first top), declines to a trough, then rises again to approximately the same price level (second top) but fails to break higher. Price then declines decisively below the trough level between the two peaks. A horizontal line may be drawn at the peak level to emphasize the resistance.]*

#### DOUBLE BOTTOM
- A double bottom appears when a share hits a low, comes higher, again pulls back.
- A double bottom appears at the end of a bearish trend and indicates the start of the bullish trend.
- Price Trend → First Trough → Peak → Second Trough → Advance From Trough → Resistance Break → Resistance Turned Support → Price Target.
*[Illustration Description: A price chart forming a "W" shape. Price declines to a trough (first bottom), rises to a peak, then declines again to approximately the same price level (second bottom) but fails to break lower. Price then rises decisively above the peak level between the two troughs. A horizontal line may be drawn at the trough level to emphasize the support. Labels may show the progression: first trough, peak, second trough, breakout, and projected price target.]*

## STRATEGIES

### GAPS
- Gaps occur when a price opens much higher (gap higher) or lower (gap lower) than the previous day's close.
- Once a gap occurs, the new price represents an important price level. Gaps higher create support that should allow the stock to move higher and gaps lower create resistance that should pressure the stock lower.
- Until the gap is violated, we should assume the trend will continue in the gap's direction.

*[Illustration Description: A generic gap illustration showing two consecutive candlesticks/bar charts with a visible vertical space between them where no trading occurred. An upward gap shows the second candle opening above the prior candle's high; a downward gap shows the second candle opening below the prior candle's low. The empty space between is labeled "Gap".]*

#### BREAKAWAY GAPS
- They occur when the price action is breaking out of their trading range or congestion area.
- A congestion area is just a price range in which the market has traded for some period of time, usually a few weeks or so.
- To break out of these areas requires market enthusiasm and, either, many more buyers than sellers for upside breakouts or more sellers than buyers for downside breakouts.

*[Illustration Description: A chart showing a horizontal consolidation zone (price moving sideways within parallel support and resistance lines). At the right edge, a large gap appears with price jumping above the resistance line (for bullish breakout) or below the support line (for bearish breakout). Volume bars below the price chart may show a spike at the breakout point.]*

#### RUNAWAY GAPS
- Runaway gaps are also called measuring gaps, and are best described as gaps that are caused by increased interest in the stock.
- For runaway gaps to the upside, it usually represents traders who did not get in during the initial move of the up trend and while waiting for a retracement in price, decided it was not going to happen.
- Increased buying interest happens all of a sudden, and the price gaps above the previous day's close. This type of runaway gap represents an almost panic state in traders.

*[Illustration Description: A chart showing a strong existing uptrend. Midway through the trend, a gap appears with price jumping significantly higher than the prior close, continuing the upward momentum. The gap is labeled "Runaway/Measuring Gap". A projection line may show the gap occurring roughly halfway through the total trend move.]*

#### EXHAUSTION GAPS
- Exhaustion gaps are those that happen near the end of a good up- or downtrend. They are many times the first signal of the end of that move.
- They are identified by high volume and large price difference between the previous day's close and the new opening price. They can easily be mistaken for runaway gaps if one does not notice the exceptionally high volume.

*[Illustration Description: A chart showing a prolonged uptrend nearing its peak. A large upward gap appears, but immediately after the gap, price stalls and reverses downward. Below the price chart, a volume indicator shows an unusually high spike coinciding with the gap day, labeled "Exceptionally High Volume". This contrasts with the runaway gap illustration by showing the reversal after the gap.]*

### ROUNDING BOTTOM
- The Rounding Bottom is a long-term reversal pattern that is best suited for weekly charts.
- It is also referred to as a saucer bottom, and represents a long consolidation period that turns from a bearish bias to a bullish bias.

*[Illustration Description: A price chart showing a gradual U-shaped or saucer-shaped curve. Price declines slowly, flattens at the bottom over an extended period, then gradually rises back up. The curve is smooth and symmetrical, resembling a bowl. The left side of the "U" represents the downtrend; the bottom represents consolidation; the right side represents the emerging uptrend.]*

### THE CUP AND THE HANDLE
- The Cup with Handle is a bullish continuation pattern that marks a consolidation period followed by a breakout.
- As its name implies, there are two parts to the pattern: the cup and the handle.
- The cup forms after an advance and looks like a bowl or rounding bottom.
- As the cup is completed, a trading range develops on the right hand side and the handle is formed.
- A subsequent breakout from the handle's trading range signals a continuation of the prior advance.

*[Illustration Description: A price chart showing two distinct formations. First, a large U-shaped "cup" similar to the rounding bottom. Immediately following the right side of the cup, a smaller downward-sloping or sideways consolidation forms—the "handle"—typically retracing about one-third of the cup's advance. A breakout above the handle's resistance is shown with an arrow, indicating the continuation of the prior uptrend.]*

Candlesticks or bars display Open, High, Low, Close prices. The chart may highlight a cup-and-handle or other pattern discussed, with annotations marking key price levels and pattern components.]*

## TRIANGLES
- A triangle is formed when each succeeding peak is lower than the previous peak
- Or each succeeding bottom is higher than the previous bottom
- The series of peaks and bottoms are joined by a line which converges and form a shape of triangle.

### SYMMETRIC TRIANGLE
*[Illustration Description: A price chart with two converging trendlines. The upper trendline connects a series of lower highs (descending resistance); the lower trendline connects a series of higher lows (ascending support). The two lines converge toward a point on the right, forming a symmetrical triangle. Price oscillates within the triangle with decreasing volatility. A breakout arrow may show price eventually breaking above or below the triangle.]*

### ASCENDING TRIANGLES (BULLISH)
*[Illustration Description: A price chart with a horizontal upper trendline connecting multiple peaks at approximately the same price level (flat resistance). The lower trendline connects a series of higher lows (ascending support), sloping upward. The two lines converge to the right. Price tests the horizontal resistance repeatedly before eventually breaking upward, indicated by an arrow.]*

### DESCENDING TRIANGLE (BEARISH)
*[Illustration Description: A price chart with a horizontal lower trendline connecting multiple troughs at approximately the same price level (flat support). The upper trendline connects a series of lower highs (descending resistance), sloping downward. The two lines converge to the right. Price tests the horizontal support repeatedly before eventually breaking downward, indicated by an arrow.]*

## FLAGS
- A flag pattern appears when a bully rally or a bear phase is interrupted by a consolidation pattern appearing as a rectangle or a parallelogram.
- As the flag formation indicates a pause before continuation of earlier trend, the prices move in the same direction after the flag as before.

### PARALLELOGRAM FLAG
*[Illustration Description: A price chart showing a sharp, nearly vertical price move (the "flagpole"). Following this, a small rectangular or parallelogram-shaped consolidation forms, with parallel trendlines sloping slightly against the prior trend direction (e.g., slightly downward after an uptrend). Price then breaks out of the flag in the original direction, continuing the prior move. The entire formation resembles a flag on a pole.]*

## INDICATOR ANALYSIS
- It's a mathematical examination of price and volume information over a given period.
- **Objective:** To predict where and in which direction the price may move in near future
- Attempts to establish a mathematical relationship of current price past prices.

### MOVING AVERAGES
- It refers to average level of closing prices, calculated on regular basis.
- A sequence of averages is calculated by calculating averages on daily basis.
*[Illustration Description: A price chart with one or more smooth curved lines overlaid on the price bars/candlesticks. Each line represents a moving average (e.g., 50-day, 200-day). The moving average lines lag behind price action and smooth out short-term fluctuations. Crossovers between different moving averages or between price and a moving average may be highlighted.]*

### RELATIVE STRENGTH INDEX
- Developed by J. Welles Wilder, the Relative Strength Index (RSI) is a momentum oscillator that measures the speed and change of price movements.
- **Formula:** RSI = 100 - 100/(1 + RS*)
  - Where RS = average of x days' up closes / average of x days' down closes.

*[Illustration Description: A two-panel chart. The top panel shows the price chart of a security. The bottom panel shows the RSI oscillator as a line fluctuating between 0 and 100. Two horizontal reference lines are drawn at 70 (overbought threshold) and 30 (oversold threshold). The RSI line crossing above 70 or below 30 is highlighted, potentially with labels indicating "Overbought" or "Oversold".]*

### CROSSOVER
- Crossover is the point on a stock chart when a security and an indicator intersect.
- Crossovers are used by technical analysts to aid in forecasting the future movements in the price of a stock.
*[Illustration Description: A chart showing two lines: one representing price (or a short-term moving average) and another representing an indicator (or a long-term moving average). The point where the two lines intersect is circled or marked with an arrow and labeled "Crossover". A bullish crossover (short-term crossing above long-term) and/or bearish crossover (short-term crossing below long-term) may be shown.]*

## WEAKNESSES OF TECHNICAL ANALYSIS
- **Experience:** Careful identification and interpretation of pattern requires a lot of experience.
- **Biasness:** Must be free from biasness of technical analyst.
- **Quickness in identification of a pattern:** The technical analyst must be a quick identifier of the pattern.
- **Long term perspective:** Emphasis in the technical analysis should always be on the long term pattern.
- **Not suitable in case of new listings**
- **Cannot forecast new phenomenon** (like 2008 financial crisis).

- **Support** is the price level at which demand is thought to be strong enough to prevent the price from declining further.

  - The logic dictates that as the price advances towards resistance, sellers become more inclined to sell and buyers become less inclined to buy.

• Gaps occur when a price opens much higher (gap higher) or

continue in the gap’s direction.
