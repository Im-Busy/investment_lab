Page 2: Abstract: Pair trading remains a cornerstone strategy in quantitative finance, having consistently
Page 2: attracted scholarly attention from both economists and computer scientists. Over recent decades,
Page 2: research has expanded beyond traditional linear frameworksΓÇösuch as regression- and
Page 2: cointegration-based modelsΓÇöto embrace advanced methodologies, including machine learning
Page 2: (ML), deep learning (DL), reinforcement learning (RL), and deep reinforcement learning (DRL).
Page 2: These techniques have demonstrated superior capacity to capture nonlinear dependencies and
Page 2: complex dynamics in financial data, thereby enhancing predictive performance and strategy
Page 2: design.
Page 2: Building on these academic developments, practitioners are increasingly deploying DL models to
Page 2: forecast asset price movements and volatility in equity and foreign exchange markets, leveraging
Page 2: the advantages of artificial intelligence (AI) for trading. In parallel, DRL has gained prominence
Page 2: in algorithmic trading, where agents can autonomously learn optimal trading policies by
Page 2: interacting with market environments, enabling systems that move beyond price prediction to
Page 2: dynamic signal generation and portfolio allocation.
Page 2: This paper provides a comprehensive survey of ML-, DL-, RL-, and DRL-based approaches to
Page 2: pair trading within quantitative finance. By systematically reviewing existing studies and
Page 2: highlighting their methodological contributions, it offers researchers a structured foundation for
Page 2: replication and further development. In addition, the paper outlines promising avenues for future
Page 2: research that extend the application of AI-driven methods in statistical arbitrage and market
Page 2: microstructure analysis.

Page 3: 1. Introduction
Page 3: Stock market forecasting concerns the prediction of future values of company shares or other
Page 3: financial assets traded on exchanges. While accurate forecasts can generate substantial financial
Page 3: gains, the task remains inherently complex. According to the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH),
Page 3: stock prices incorporate all publicly available information, implying that future movements cannot
Page 3: be reliably inferred from historical prices alone, as they are primarily driven by new information.
Page 3: Consequently, models that depend exclusively on past prices tend to have limited predictive power,
Page 3: especially given marketsΓÇÖ high sensitivity to external shocks.
Page 3: The proliferation of the Internet has introduced alternative sources of collective
Page 3: intelligenceΓÇösuch as Google Trends and Wikipedia usageΓÇöthat may capture shifts in investor
Page 3: attention and sentiment. This has led to the view that prices are shaped not only by financial news
Page 3: but also by real-time public interest. Hence, a central question arises: to what extent can historical
Page 3: price data, possibly enhanced with such alternative sources, be used to improve forecasting
Page 3: accuracy?
Page 3: Early investigations into the predictability of stock markets were deeply rooted in theories
Page 3: like Early investigations into market predictability were grounded in the EMH and the random
Page 3: walk hypothesis, which posit that prices adjust randomly to new information and therefore cannot
Page 3: be systematically predicted from past data. Under these frameworks, forecasting stock returns was
Page 3: regarded as no better than random guessing. Nevertheless, subsequent research has increasingly
Page 3: challenged these assumptions, suggesting that financial markets may display patterns that enable
Page 3: partial predictability. The sustained outperformance of certain investors, such as Warren Buffett,
Page 3: is often cited as suggestive evidence that markets are not perfectly efficient.
Page 3: Developing robust forecasting models, however, remains difficult because asset prices are
Page 3: influenced by a wide range of factors, including firm fundamentals, macroeconomic conditions,
Page 3: market sentiment, and historical dynamics. Models based on a single predictor typically fail to
Page 3: capture this complexity, whereas incorporating multiple featuresΓÇösuch as news sentiment, social
Page 3: media activity, and technical indicatorsΓÇöcan improve predictive performance by reflecting the
Page 3: multifaceted drivers of market behavior.

Page 5: Among statistical arbitrage approaches, pairs trading has emerged as one of the most widely
Page 5: studied and implemented strategies. The basic idea is to identify pairs of assets whose prices have
Page 5: historically moved together and to exploit temporary deviations in their relative valuation. When
Page 5: the spread between two assets diverges beyond a predefined threshold, traders take offsetting
Page 5: positions with the expectation that the spread will revert to its long-run equilibrium. Despite its
Page 5: intuitive appeal, the strategy involves several practical challenges, including robust pair selection,
Page 5: optimal threshold determination, and accurate timing of trade execution.
Page 5: Recent advances in ML, DL, and RL have the potential to substantially enhance pairs trading.
Page 5: ML algorithms can process large-scale datasets to uncover nonlinear dependencies and hidden
Page 5: correlations between securities that are often overlooked by traditional statistical methods. Both
Page 5: supervised and unsupervised learning have been applied, with the former forecasting spread
Page 5: dynamics and the latter clustering securities into candidate pairs. DL architectures, particularly
Page 5: recurrent neural networks (RNNs) and long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, are well-suited
Page 5: for modeling temporal dependencies and nonlinear spread behavior, while more recent approaches
Page 5: explore convolutional and transformer-based models. RL offers a different paradigm, focusing on

Page 6: Overall, these advanced AI-driven techniques promise to improve the robustness and
Page 6: adaptability of pairs trading strategies. By enabling models that learn from data and adjust in real
Page 6: time, ML, DL, and RL provide new opportunities for capturing complex dynamics in increasingly
Page 6: data-rich and competitive financial markets. This motivates a growing body of literature that
Page 6: applies AI methods to pairs trading, which the present survey systematically reviews.

Page 7: 2. Related Work
Page 7: This section reviews the evolution of ML methodologies applied to pairs trading, illustrating
Page 7: how computational approaches have progressively transformed strategy design and
Page 7: implementation. Early studies predominantly relied on unsupervised learning techniquesΓÇösuch
Page 7: as clustering and cointegration analysisΓÇöto identify candidate trading pairs based on historical
Page 7: co-movements. While effective in capturing broad similarities, these approaches were limited by
Page 7: their inability to incorporate predictive signals or adapt to changing market dynamics.
Page 7: The subsequent introduction of supervised learning methods addressed some of these
Page 7: shortcomings by leveraging labeled financial data to refine pair selection and improve the timing
Page 7: of trade execution. With the advent of DL, researchers further advanced pairs trading by modeling
Page 7: nonlinear dependencies and temporal structures in financial time series, employing architectures
Page 7: such as recurrent and convolutional neural networks. More recently, RL has emerged
Page 7: as a promising paradigm, enabling adaptive strategy optimization through continuous interaction
Page 7: with market environments and dynamic adjustment of entryΓÇôexit rules.
Page 7: Collectively, these methodological advances highlight the central role of ML in enhancing
Page 7: the robustness, adaptability, and profitability of pairs trading strategies, while also signaling
Page 7: a broader transition toward data-driven decision-making in financial markets.
Page 7: 2.1 Unsupervised Learning Methods
Page 7: Unsupervised learning methods uncover latent patterns and structural relationships in data
Page 7: without relying on labeled outcomes. In financial markets, these techniques are particularly useful
Page 7: for detecting correlations and dependencies that arise naturally across assets, making them well
Page 7: suited for identifying candidate pairs in statistical arbitrage strategies.

Page 8: relevance; and (iii) methodological novelty, especially where machine learning (ML) techniques

Page 8: Chen et al. (2012) Chen et al. (2012) investigate the application of machine learning
Page 8: techniques to enhance pairs trading, a statistical arbitrage strategy that exploits mean-reverting
Page 8: spreads between correlated assets. The study compares two modeling approaches: a conventional
Page 8: portfolio rebalancing and linear regression framework, and a more sophisticated method that
Page 8: integrates Kalman filtering with the ExpectationΓÇôMaximization (EM) algorithm. Using data from
Page 8: the China futures market, the empirical results indicate that while both approaches show potential,
Page 8: the Kalman filterΓÇôbased model delivers superior profitability. This work illustrates the benefits of
Page 8: incorporating adaptive, data-driven methods into traditional arbitrage frameworks, particularly in
Page 8: capturing dynamic co-movement between assets.
Page 8: Sohail et al. (2020) apply the Density-Based Spatial Clustering of Applications with Noise
Page 8: (DBSCAN) algorithm in combination with conventional pairs trading techniques to identify
Page 8: structurally similar stocks using firm size (market capitalization) and principal component analysis
Page 8: (PCA) of daily returns. Using data from the Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX), the study
Page 8: demonstrates that this ML-enhanced approach generates statistically significant average monthly
Page 8: excess returns while preserving market neutrality and confirming mean-reversion dynamics.

Page 8: particularly within the context of emerging markets.

Page 9: 2.2 Supervised Learning Methods
Page 9: This section examines supervised learning methods, where models are trained on historical
Page 9: datasets with labeled outcomes to predict future events. Such techniques are increasingly applied
Page 9: to pair trading, where accurate forecasting is critical for optimizing entry and exit decisions.
Page 9: By learning from past market behavior, supervised models can uncover patterns that inform both
Page 9: the timing and selection of trading pairs, thereby improving the precision and potential profitability
Page 9: of arbitrage strategies.
Page 9: In the context of pair trading, supervised learning has been instrumental in developing
Page 9: predictive models that assess the likelihood of mean reversion and estimate the expected duration
Page 9: of spread convergence. Recent studies demonstrate that algorithms such as decision trees, support
Page 9: vector machines, and neural networks can capture complex, nonlinear relationships within
Page 9: financial time series. These models not only contribute to improved trading performance but also
Page 9: offer enhanced robustness under dynamic and volatile market conditions. Nonetheless, challenges
Page 9: remainΓÇöparticularly regarding the construction of appropriate labels (e.g., defining convergence
Page 9: events) and addressing class imbalance in financial datasetsΓÇöwhich require careful
Page 9: methodological design.

Page 11: 2.3 Deep Learning Methods
Page 11: This review next turns to DL methodsΓÇöa specialized subset of ML that employs DNNs to
Page 11: capture complex, nonlinear patterns in large-scale datasets. In the context of pair trading, DL has
Page 11: emerged as a transformative tool, offering refined insights into market dynamics and enabling the
Page 11: development of more adaptive and sophisticated trading algorithms.

Page 12: For clarity, DRL is not treated here as an isolated topic but rather as the intersection of DL
Page 12: and RL. RL refers to a class of ML methods in which an agent interacts with an environment and
Page 12: learns optimal actions through trial and error, guided by rewards and penalties to maximize
Page 12: cumulative returns. DL, in contrast, leverages multilayer neural networks to automatically extract
Page 12: high-level features from raw inputs, and has demonstrated strong capabilities in domains such as
Page 12: computer vision and natural language processing.
Page 12: DRL combines the representational power of DL with the sequential decision-making ability
Page 12: of RL by using neural networks to approximate value functions, policies, or transition models.
Page 12: Prominent DRL algorithms such as Deep Q-Networks (DQN), Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient
Page 12: (DDPG), and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) exemplify this integration. This allows DRL to
Page 12: handle high-dimensional input spacesΓÇösuch as multivariate time series in financial marketsΓÇö
Page 12: supporting end-to-end learning directly from raw data.
Page 12: In financial applications, including pairs trading, DRL provides the flexibility to adaptively
Page 12: optimize trading policies in dynamic and uncertain environments where traditional RL methods
Page 12: face scalability constraints. Although applications of DRL to pairs trading remain relatively limited,
Page 12: its ability to integrate feature extraction and policy learning presents a promising frontier for future
Page 12: research.

Page 12: robust predictive modeling.

Page 12: predictive power of pairs trading. Through empirical application, the study illustrates the modelΓÇÖs
Page 12: capacity to capture complex relationships between financial assets, offering a more nuanced and

Page 21: 4.2 Historical Price Data with Tick Interval
Page 21: Tick-level historical data represents the most granular form of market information, capturing
Page 21: every individual transaction executed on an exchange. Unlike intraday datasets aggregated over
Page 21: fixed intervals (e.g., 1-minute or 5-minute bars), tick data provides a continuous stream of timestamped records, each detailing the exact execution time, trade price, and trade size.
Page 21: This high-resolution dataset is particularly valuable for HFT strategies, which rely on
Page 21: executing a large number of trades within milliseconds or seconds. Researchers and practitioners
Page 21: use tick data to construct fine-grained models of market behavior, enabling the detection of shortterm price patterns and microstructure dynamics with high precision. Moreover, tick data supports
Page 21:

Page 22: processing speed, and computational requirements, due to the massive volume of records
Page 22: generated. Nonetheless, for algorithmic and intraday traders, the predictive power of tick data often
Page 22: justifies these computational demands, particularly in markets characterized by rapid price

Page 22: 4.3 Historical Price Data with One-Minute Interval
Page 22: One-minute historical data represents a practical compromise between the ultra-high
Page 22: granularity of tick-level data and the broader perspective of hourly or daily intervals. Each record
Page 22: captures the OHLC prices and trading volume for a specific minute, thereby offering a more
Page 22: detailed view of market activity than daily data while avoiding the overwhelming scale of tick data.
Page 22: 
Page 22:

Page 23: 
Page 23: 21
Page 23: 
Page 23: This resolution is particularly well-suited for medium-frequency trading strategies, as it
Page 23: enables analysts and traders to track short-term price dynamics and assess evolving market
Page 23: conditions with enhanced clarity. At the minute level, researchers can investigate phenomena such
Page 23: as volatility clustering, price momentum, and short-term reversals, all of which are essential for
Page 23: intraday decision-making and predictive modeling.
Page 23: Although one-minute data is less demanding than tick-level data, it still requires considerable
Page 23: computational resources and analytical sophistication, especially when applied to large crosssectional datasets or extended time horizons. Nevertheless, it strikes a valuable balance between

Page 26: 4.6 Historical Price Data with Monthly Interval
Page 26: Monthly historical data provide a broad, long-term perspective on market performance by
Page 26: aggregating trading activity over one-month intervals. Each record typically includes the opening
Page 26: price at the start of the month, the highest and lowest prices during the period, the closing price
Page 26: at month-end, and the total trading volume. In addition, monthly datasets generally contain
Page 26: the adjusted closing price, which incorporates dividends and stock splits to provide a more accurate
Page 26: measure of long-term returns. Compared with high-frequency or intraday data, monthly data are
Page 26: less granular but offer valuable insights for strategic investment decisions and macroeconomic
Page 26: analysis.
Page 26: This type of data is particularly beneficial for long-term investors, as it facilitates
Page 26: the detection of seasonal patterns, responses to quarterly earnings announcements, and
Page 26: the influence of broader economic cycles. Analysts frequently employ monthly datasets to assess
Page 26: the overall direction and health of stocks or indices, forming the basis for portfolio rebalancing,
Page 26: asset allocation, and risk management over extended horizons.
Page 26: Although the volume of data is significantly reduced relative to tick or daily frequencies,
Page 26: monthly data still require a robust analytical framework to capture cyclical and trend-driven
Page 26: dynamics. The lower frequency mitigates short-term market noise, making it a suitable input for
Page 26: models that emphasize stability, directionality, and macro-level indicators. However, in the context
Page 26: of pairs trading, monthly data are rarely employed for direct signal generation, since trading
Page 26: opportunities typically unfold at higher frequencies. Instead, they are more commonly used to
Page 26: validate long-run equilibrium relationships or to support cross-market and cross-asset hedging
Page 26: strategies.

Page 29: two historically correlated assets and exploiting temporary deviations in their relative valuations.

Page 29: Machine Learning in Pairs Trading
Page 29: Machine learning plays a central role in identifying suitable asset pairs for statistical
Page 29: arbitrage by analyzing historical data to uncover stable, long-term relationships. The primary
Page 29: mechanism involves modeling the typical spread behavior between two assets and detecting
Page 29: significant deviations from historical norms. A critical step is feature engineering, in which inputs
Page 29: such as price ratios, price differences, volatility measures, and mean-reversion indicators are
Page 29: constructed. These features are then fed into classification or regression modelsΓÇösuch as SVM,
Page 29: LR, or RFΓÇöwhich are trained to predict the likelihood of convergence or divergence in the asset
Page 29: pair. This data-driven approach moves beyond rigid econometric assumptions, enabling traders to
Page 29: make more adaptive and statistically grounded decisions.
Page 29: Deep Learning in Pairs Trading
Page 29: Deep learning extends the capabilities of ML by capturing nonlinear and complex
Page 29: dependencies in financial data. DNNs, CNNs, and recurrent architectures such as LSTMs are
Page 29: particularly effective at identifying subtle patterns that traditional linear models fail to capture.
Page 29: In data-rich environments, DL models excel at processing large-scale, high-dimensional inputs to
Page 29: uncover latent structures that signal profitable trading opportunities. Moreover, DL methods are
Page 29: useful for anomaly detection, flagging unusual deviations in price relationships that may indicate
Page 29: trading signals. By learning from the broader distribution of market behavior, DL models enhance
Page 29: robustness, reduce false positives, and improve the reliability of signal generation in pairs trading.
Page 29: Reinforcement Learning in Pairs Trading
Page 29: Reinforcement learning introduces a fundamentally different paradigm, making it highly
Page 29: suitable for sequential decision-making in dynamic and uncertain markets. In the context of pairs
Page 29: trading, RL agents interact with a simulated or real trading environment, receiving rewards based
Page 29: on the profitability of their actions. Through this trial-and-error process, the agent progressively

Page 30: is a key advantage: agents can update strategies incrementally as new data arrive, reducing the need
Page 30: for complete retraining when market conditions evolve. Furthermore, RL frameworks facilitate
Page 30: continuous improvement through simulation and back-testing, allowing agents to be trained across
Page 30: diverse market scenarios without incurring real financial risk. This makes RL particularly effective
Page 30: in addressing non-stationarity and evolving asset relationshipsΓÇöpersistent challenges in financial
Page 30: markets.

Page 38: 5.1.1.1 Introduction to Support Vector Machine
Page 38: SVM is a widely used and versatile supervised learning algorithm that can be applied to both
Page 38: classification and regression tasks, although it is predominantly utilized for classification.
Page 38: The fundamental objective of SVM is to identify an optimal decision boundary, or hyperplane,
Page 38: that separates data points belonging to different classes with the greatest possible margin. The data
Page 38: points that lie closest to this hyperplane are referred to as support vectors, and they play a critical
Page 38: role in determining its orientation and position.
Page 38: The principle of margin maximization lies at the core of SVM. By selecting the hyperplane
Page 38: that maximizes the distance to the nearest support vectors, the algorithm enhances its ability to
Page 38: generalize to unseen data, thereby reducing the risk of overfitting. For linearly separable data, this
Page 38: hyperplane takes the form of a line in two dimensions, a plane in three dimensions, or a higherdimensional analogue. However, financial data and other real-world datasets are rarely linearly
Page 38: separable. To address this limitation, SVM employs the kernel trick, which maps the original
Page 38: feature space into a higher-dimensional one where linear separation becomes feasible. This
Page 38: transformation is performed implicitly by kernel functions, avoiding the computational burden of
Page 38: explicit feature expansion. Commonly used kernels include the polynomial kernel, the radial basis
Page 38: function (RBF) kernel, and the sigmoid kernel, each offering different capacities to capture
Page 38: nonlinear structures in the data.
Page 38: An additional and equally important aspect of SVM is regularization, which is governed by
Page 38: a penalty parameter commonly denoted as ≡¥É╢ . This parameter balances the trade-off between
Page 38: maximizing the margin and minimizing classification errors on the training data. A large value of
Page 38: ≡¥É╢ forces the model to prioritize correct classification of training samples, often at the expense of
Page 38: generalization, whereas a smaller value of ≡¥É╢ allows some misclassifications but typically yields
Page 38: a more robust model. This flexibility is particularly relevant in financial applications, where noise
Page 38: and outliers are common in market data.

Page 42: 5.1.2 Unsupervised Learning
Page 42: Unsupervised learning is a type of ML in which algorithms are trained using input data
Page 42: without corresponding output labels. The primary objective is to uncover the underlying structure
Page 42: or distribution of the data, thereby gaining insights into hidden patterns and relationships. This
Page 42: paradigm is particularly useful when the outcomes are unknown in advance and the goal is
Page 42: exploratory analysis rather than direct prediction.
Page 42: Unsupervised learning tasks are typically categorized into clustering and dimensionality
Page 42: reduction. Clustering algorithms, such as k-means or DBSCAN, aim to identify inherent groups
Page 42: within the data, assigning unlabeled points to meaningful subgroups based on similarity. This
Page 42: process is widely applied in finance for tasks such as grouping assets with similar dynamics or risk
Page 42: profiles. Dimensionality reduction techniques, most notably PCA, serve to project highdimensional financial data into lower-dimensional representations while retaining the most
Page 42: informative variance components. Although association rule mining is also a branch of
Page 42: unsupervised learning, its applications are more prevalent in domains such as retail and
Page 42: recommendation systems rather than financial markets.

Page 42: are the dominant unsupervised approaches adopted. Specifically, DBSCAN has been implemented
Page 42: in four studies, accounting for approximately 6% of applications, while PCA has been applied in
Page 42: two studies, representing a smaller proportion. Although their overall usage is limited compared
Page 42: to supervised learning models, these methods play an important role in enhancing pairs trading
Page 42: strategies. DBSCAN contributes by identifying asset clusters with strong co-movement properties,
Page 42: while PCA facilitates noise reduction and the extraction of latent factors driving asset returns.

Page 46: Practical Implementation Summarize
Page 46: In practical implementation, the process begins with data preparation. This involves
Page 46: collecting and preprocessing historical price data for a broad set of stocks, ensuring that the dataset
Page 46: is clean and consistent for analysis. Once the data is prepared, attention shifts to parameter
Page 46: selection. The two key parameters of DBSCANΓÇöthe neighborhood radius (epsilon) and the
Page 46: minimum number of points required to form a dense region (min_samples)ΓÇömust be carefully
Page 46: tuned, as they critically determine the clustering results and, by extension, the effectiveness of the
Page 46: trading strategy.
Page 46: Following parameter calibration, DBSCAN is executed on the processed dataset to identify
Page 46: meaningful stock clusters. These clusters are then examined to select potential trading pairs based
Page 46: on their historical co-movements. To validate the strategyΓÇÖs performance, back-testing is
Page 46: conducted using historical data, evaluating both feasibility and profitability of the identified
Page 46: trading opportunities.

Page 55: 5.3.1 Introduction to Deep Q-network
Page 55: RL operates through the interaction between an agent, a set of states ≡¥æå, and a set of possible
Page 55: actions ≡¥É┤ that the agent can take in each state. When the agent performs an action ≡¥æÄ Γêê ≡¥É┤ ,
Page 55: it transitions to a new state and receives a reward, a numerical signal that reflects the immediate
Page 55: utility of that action. The ultimate objective of the agent is to learn a policy that maximizes
Page 55: cumulative rewards over time.
Page 55: To achieve this goal, the agent must evaluate the potential future rewards that may result
Page 55: from its current decisions. These rewards are typically represented as the expected value of
Page 55: discounted returns from subsequent states, allowing the agent to balance immediate gains against
Page 55: long-term benefits. One of the most widely used algorithms in this context is Q-learning, a modelfree RL technique designed to estimate the value of taking a particular action in a given state.
Page 55: The framework of Q-learning is illustrated in Figure 6.
Page 55: A key advantage of Q-learning is that it does not require prior knowledge of
Page 55: the environmentΓÇÖs transition dynamicsΓÇöhence the term model-free. It can effectively handle
Page 55:

Page 56: a partially random policy, it is guaranteed to converge to the optimal policy in any finite MDP.
Page 56: At its core, Q-learning relies on the computation of a Q-function, which estimates the expected
Page 56: cumulative reward of performing an action in a given state and then following the optimal policy
Page 56: thereafter.
Page 56: While Q-learning performs well in small, discrete stateΓÇôaction spaces, it faces significant
Page 56: challenges in high-dimensional or continuous domains due to its reliance on lookup tables. In such
Page 56: settings, maintaining and updating a complete Q-table becomes computationally infeasible. This
Page 56: limitation motivated the use of function approximation, where a parameterized function ≡¥æä(≡¥æá, ≡¥æÄ) is
Page 56: employed to estimate Q-values. Here, ≡¥£â denotes the parameters of the function, typically learned

Page 56: significantly enhances the scalability of Q-learning, enabling it to learn policies directly from raw,
Page 56: high-dimensional input dataΓÇösuch as images or long sequencesΓÇöwithout explicit feature

Page 64: 6.2 Performance of Evaluation Metrics
Page 64: Evaluating the performance of an investment portfolio is a critical step in the investment
Page 64: decision-making process. The primary dimensions of this evaluation are return and risk, which
Page 64: together form the foundation for assessing portfolio outcomes. Within these dimensions, a variety
Page 64: of specific indicators have been employed in the literature to capture different aspects of portfolio
Page 64: behavior.
Page 64: Table 19 summarizes the most frequently adopted performance and risk measures. Returnbased indicators, such as Accumulated Return and Annual Return, provide a direct measure of
Page 64: profitability, while risk-adjusted measures such as the Sharpe ratio and Sortino ratio account for
Page 64: the variability of returns, with the latter placing greater emphasis on downside risk. On the risk
Page 64: dimension, Maximum Drawdown (MDD) captures the worst peak-to-trough decline over
Page 64: the investment horizon, making it particularly relevant for assessing vulnerability during market
Page 64: downturns. Volatility (standard deviation) measures overall variability, whereas higher-moment
Page 64: statistics such as Skewness and Kurtosis provide insights into the asymmetry and tail risks of return
Page 64: distributions.
Page 64: Taken together, these indicators enable a comprehensive assessment of portfolio
Page 64: performance, balancing the dual objectives of maximizing returns while controlling for risk.

Page 66: 8. Empirical Result Analysis and Future Research Direction
Page 66: 8.1 Empirical Result Analysis of Pair Trading
Page 66: Algorithmic pairs trading has become a mainstream technique in quantitative finance,
Page 66: attracting increasing participation from both institutional and individual investors. This
Page 66: intensifying competition has made arbitrage opportunities more difficult to exploit, underscoring
Page 66: the necessity of developing effective approaches to ensure sustainable profitability [14]. A review
Page 66: of the literature indicates that pairs trading remains a viable and, in many cases, profitable strategy,
Page 66: although a minority of studies report diminishing returns in more recent years.
Page 66: Recent advances in ML have substantially enhanced the performance of pairs trading
Page 66: strategies. Neural networks, by capturing non-linear dependencies and uncovering complex
Page 66: patterns, significantly improve the predictive accuracy of trading signals [1]. Empirical results
Page 66: suggest that LSTM models achieve superior accuracy across most metrics, particularly in
Page 66: identifying positive arbitrage opportunities, and generate the highest annualized return on
Page 66: investment (ROI). CNNs, while less effective on arbitrage samples, perform better in detecting
Page 66: non-arbitrage situations, exhibiting higher precision and F-measure scores. LR tends to deliver
Page 66: relatively balanced outcomes across different categories. XGBoost, despite identifying fewer
Page 66: opportunities, achieves the highest positive average ROI, suggesting stronger performance in
Page 66: arbitrage timing. Overall, ML-based strategies consistently outperform traditional cointegrationbased approaches in both profitability and predictive power [14].
Page 66: Beyond supervised ML models, reinforcement learning and optimization frameworks have
Page 66: also demonstrated promise. For example, combining the Entropy Optimization Criterion (EOC)
Page 66: for pair selection with MADDPG for trading thresholds has yielded notable improvements in
Page 66: the Chinese futures market. Simulations show that Dynamic Q-Network-based pair selection
Page 66:

Page 67: improved cumulative returns by 50% relative to static benchmarks, while the EOC method added
Page 67: a further 23% improvement. Integrating MADDPG enhanced performance by an additional
Page 67: 5ΓÇô15%, and combining both EOC and MADDPG resulted in a 29% improvement over static
Page 67: methods and 16% over DQN alone [3]. These findings highlight the potential of advanced
Page 67: learning-based approaches to significantly optimize pairs trading outcomes.
Page 67: TCN also exhibit stronger predictive ability compared with traditional financial algorithms,
Page 67: translating into substantial profit gains in trading. Further extensions, such as Knowledge-Driven
Page 67: TCN (KDTCN) and Temporal Convolutional Attention Networks (TCAN), which incorporate

Page 67: a fruitful direction for future research [28].

Page 67: trading has significantly declined since 2003, even when fundamental similarity is considered [4].
Page 67: These findings imply that market efficiency may have eroded traditional arbitrage opportunities.
Page 67: Moreover, the costs associated with trading spurious pairs remain substantial, reinforcing
Page 67: the importance of integrating fundamental information in order to identify reliable pairs.
Page 67: 8.2 Future Research Direction
Page 67: The domain of algorithmic pairs trading continues to evolve rapidly, driven by advances in
Page 67: ML and DRL. While existing studies demonstrate that these methods can significantly improve
Page 67: predictive accuracy and profitability, future research must go beyond incremental refinements and
Page 67: systematically address the challenges posed by increasingly complex data environments,
Page 67: methodological limitations, and dynamic financial markets. A forward-looking research agenda
Page 67: can be organized into four interconnected dimensions: data-driven innovations, methodological
Page 67: innovations, market applications, and governance and validation frameworks.
Page 67: Advances in ML and DRL offer substantial opportunities for refining pairs trading strategies.
Page 67: The application of advanced anomaly detection techniques to financial time series could strengthen
Page 67: risk management by identifying irregular patterns, flash crashes, or manipulative behaviors in real
Page 67: time. Another critical frontier is the development of adaptive models that adjust automatically to
Page 67: shifting market regimes, incorporating signals related to volatility, liquidity, and investor
Page 67: sentiment. Such dynamic models could enhance robustness across different market conditions,
Page 67: 
Page 67:

Page 68: including crises. Reinforcement learning methods can also be extended by integrating
Page 68: meta-learning approaches, enabling agents to generalize across trading environments and adapt to
Page 68: non-stationary market dynamics more efficiently. Moreover, the emergence of quantum
Page 68: computing offers a novel pathway for tackling high-dimensional optimization problems in
Page 68: portfolio construction and risk assessment, although practical implementation remains constrained
Page 68: by the immaturity of current quantum hardware. Collectively, these methodological advances
Page 68: promise significant improvements, but they also raise concerns regarding computational
Page 68: complexity, sample efficiency, and interpretability.
Page 68: Expanding pairs trading beyond equities toward cross-asset arbitrage is another promising
Page 68: direction. Identifying pricing inefficiencies between equities, commodities, currencies, and digital
Page 68: assets requires models capable of handling heterogeneous trading environments, liquidity
Page 68: conditions, and regulatory regimes. Such approaches could generate new opportunities for
Page 68: diversification and risk hedging. At the same time, the incorporation of game theory and agentbased modeling provides a valuable framework for simulating the strategic interactions of multiple
Page 68: market participants. Modeling markets as multi-agent systems may yield insights into equilibrium
Page 68: behaviors, competitive dynamics, and emergent liquidity patterns. However, cross-asset strategies
Page 68: face practical challenges related to execution, transaction costs, and heterogeneous market
Page 68: microstructures, while multi-agent frameworks often struggle with calibration and validation
Page 68: against real-world data.
Page 68: As algorithmic trading strategies become increasingly sophisticated, ethical considerations
Page 68: and regulatory compliance must be integrated into future research agendas. Developing transparent
Page 68: and auditable algorithms will not only facilitate alignment with regulatory frameworks but also
Page 68: strengthen trust in financial markets. Automated compliance monitoring systems represent one
Page 68: possible solution to ensure that trading models operate within ethical and legal boundaries.
Page 68: Furthermore, rigorous real-world validation remains essential. While back-testing provides initial
Page 68: evidence of profitability, forward-testing in simulated or paper trading environments, as well as
Page 68: stress-testing under extreme scenarios, is critical for assessing robustness and practical viability.
Page 68: Ensuring reproducibility and mitigating biasesΓÇösuch as survivorship bias or look-ahead biasΓÇö
Page 68: remain ongoing challenges that require systematic attention.

Page 69: multi-agent frameworks can broaden the scope of arbitrage opportunities. At the same time,
Page 69: embedding governance and rigorous validation practices will be essential to balance technological
Page 69: advancement with market stability and transparency.
Page 69: 9. Discussion
Page 69: This study has examined the evolution of pairs trading strategies, with particular attention to
Page 69: the growing role of AI techniques in quantitative finance. Early approaches were predominantly
Page 69: grounded in classical time-series methods, including cointegration and mean-reversion models,
Page 69: which provided a robust statistical framework for identifying arbitrage opportunities. These
Page 69: techniques, while foundational, were constrained by their linear assumptions and limited ability to
Page 69: capture the nonlinear dependencies frequently observed in financial markets.
Page 69: The development of ML methods marked a significant advancement. Algorithms such as RF
Page 69: and SVM demonstrated improved capacity to model nonlinearities and high-dimensional
Page 69: interactions. In particular, SVMΓÇÖs kernel-based transformations enabled the identification of subtle
Page 69: decision boundaries in complex data. Nevertheless, these methods often required extensive feature
Page 69: engineering and were sensitive to parameter tuning, which limited their scalability in rapidly
Page 69: changing market conditions.
Page 69: ANNs represented another turning point by offering greater flexibility in capturing latent
Page 69: structures within financial time series. Building upon this foundation, RNNs and their LSTM
Page 69: variants further improved the modeling of temporal dependencies, allowing for more accurate
Page 69: forecasts of spread dynamics. Despite these improvements, challenges such as overfitting, high
Page 69: computational costs, and limited interpretability remain critical concerns in their practical
Page 69: implementation.
Page 69: More recently, RL has introduced a dynamic framework for decision-making in pairs trading.
Page 69: By learning iteratively from market feedback, RL algorithms are capable of adapting strategies in
Page 69: response to shifting conditions, thereby offering a mechanism to balance profitability and risk in
Page 69: 
Page 69:

Page 70: 
Page 70: 68
Page 70: 
Page 70: real time. However, issues of sample efficiency, stability of training, and robustness under extreme
Page 70: market stress continue to constrain the widespread adoption of RL in live trading systems.
Page 70: A particularly promising direction is the integration of DL and RL, creating hybrid models
Page 70: that combine powerful feature extraction with adaptive decision-making. These approaches have
Page 70: shown potential in capturing complex market dynamics and improving resilience under volatile
Page 70: conditions. Yet, their reliance on vast amounts of data and computational resources, as well as
Page 70: their limited transparency, highlight important areas for future refinement.
Page 70: In summary, our analysis highlights both the potential and limitations of AI in reshaping
Page 70: pairs trading strategies. ML and DL methods significantly enhance predictive accuracy, while RL
Page 70: contributes adaptability to evolving markets. The convergence of these methods offers a powerful
Page 70: toolkit for traders; however, practical deployment requires addressing challenges of interpretability,
Page 70: computational efficiency, and robustness. Continued research into these areas is essential for
Page 70: translating methodological advances into sustainable, real-world trading strategies.
Page 70: 10. Conclusion
Page 70: This survey has sought to provide a comprehensive account of the evolution of pairs trading
Page 70: strategies, with a particular emphasis on the transformative role of ML, DL, and RL.
Page 70: By systematically reviewing the literature, we have highlighted how traditional statistical methods
Page 70: have gradually been complementedΓÇöand in some cases outperformedΓÇöby advanced
Page 70: AI techniques capable of modeling nonlinearities, capturing temporal dependencies, and
Page 70: dynamically adapting to shifting market conditions.
Page 70: From a theoretical perspective, our findings underscore the significance of ML and DL as
Page 70: foundational tools in modern quantitative finance. These technologies extend beyond linear
Page 70: assumptions, enabling the identification of latent structures and complex interactions in financial
Page 70: time series. At the same time, hybrid approaches that integrate DL and RL provide a promising
Page 70: pathway toward the development of adaptive trading strategies that can adjust to evolving market
Page 70: environments.
Page 70: On the practical side, this study provides a structured overview of evaluation metrics,
Page 70: implementation frameworks, and empirical outcomes. By synthesizing results across a broad set
Page 70: of studies, we offer a set of reference benchmarks that can inform both academic research and

Page 71: show considerable promise in improving profitability and risk management, their effectiveness is
Page 71: highly dependent on data quality, market regime, and implementation constraints.
Page 71: Nonetheless, several limitations remain. The reliance on historical data for training exposes
Page 71: models to risks of overfitting and survivorship bias, while the interpretability of complex models
Page 71: such as neural networks continues to be a major concern for both researchers and regulators.
Page 71: Furthermore, transaction costs, liquidity constraints, and market frictions may erode the theoretical
Page 71: profitability observed in back-testing environments.
Page 71: Looking forward, the field presents fertile ground for further exploration. Promising avenues

Page 71: In conclusion, while the predictive capacity of ML, DL, and RL offers unprecedented
Page 71: opportunities for enhancing pairs trading, their success in real-world applications will ultimately
Page 71: depend on striking a balance between innovation, robustness, interpretability, and regulatory
Page 71: compliance. Addressing these challenges will ensure that algorithmic pairs trading continues to
Page 71: evolve not only as a profitable strategy but also as a sustainable component of modern financial
